## How to use this rubric

Score each criterion independently. Do not let performance on one criterion
inflate or deflate scores on another. Read the full descriptor for each level
before assigning a score. Assign the level that best describes the overall
quality of the work, not the highest level the team touched on briefly.

Scores are assigned at the criterion level, not the sub-criterion level.
The point allocations within each criterion are not divided further. Assign
the full points for the level that matches the work.

## Criterion 1: Strategic Insight and Analytical Rigor
Weight: 30% | Points available: 12

This criterion assesses whether the team's analysis moves beyond description
to genuine strategic insight, whether frameworks are applied correctly and
consequentially, and whether the recommendation is specific, defensible,
and grounded in evidence from the analysis.

| Level | Points | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| Distinction | 11 to 12 | Every technology and market finding has an explicit strategic implication stated as a consequence, not a topic. Rogers five domains, Christensen classification, and Porter Five Forces are applied to the specific Georgian industry context with named evidence. The recommendation is precise: it names the client's action, the target segment or domain, the mechanism of value creation, and the timeline. Every claim in the recommendation is traceable to a specific finding in the analysis. The team can defend any number or strategic judgment under Q&A without hesitation. |
| Merit | 8 to 10 | Most findings have strategic implications stated. Frameworks are applied, not merely listed. The recommendation is specific enough to be evaluated but one or two claims lack clear evidence linkage. The team defends the core recommendation well under Q&A but struggles with one or two peripheral assumptions. The analysis is genuinely insightful in the majority of sections. |
| Pass | 5 to 7 | Frameworks are present and mostly correctly applied. Some findings have implications stated; others remain descriptive. The recommendation is identifiable but lacks precision in at least one of: target, mechanism, or timeline. The team can defend the general direction of the recommendation under Q&A but cannot defend specific numbers or assumptions with confidence. |
| Fail | 0 to 4 | Analysis describes technology or market conditions without stating strategic consequences. Frameworks are listed or named but not applied to the specific client context. The recommendation is generic, vague, or not traceable to the analysis. The team cannot defend the recommendation under Q&A or contradicts its own analysis when challenged. |

**What separates Merit from Distinction:**
A Merit team states implications. A Distinction team states implications and connects
them causally to the recommendation. Every finding should answer two questions:
so what, and therefore what?

**Q&A indicators for this criterion:**
- Number challenge: can the team answer what happens to the base case payback if the
  named adoption assumption falls 30 percent?
- Structural challenge: can the team explain what is structurally different about
  each strategic option, not just what is different in cost or scale?

## Criterion 2: Quality and Feasibility of Transformation Roadmap
Weight: 30% | Points available: 12

This criterion assesses whether the 18-month roadmap is credible, complete, and
correctly sequenced. It also assesses whether change management and governance
are treated as substantive workstreams with budgets, owners, and timelines, rather
than as risk mitigations or afterthoughts.

| Level | Points | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| Distinction | 11 to 12 | Every initiative in the roadmap contains all seven required elements: horizon tag, owner function, budget estimate, timeline, dependency, success metric, and risk. Data governance and oversight body establishment appear in months 1 to 3, before any AI deployment initiative. Change management is a named workstream with a budget at 20 to 30 percent of total transformation cost, a named owner function, and a phased activity plan. The governance plan names an oversight body with defined membership, decision rights, and review cadence, and states a specific trigger threshold and override authority for every high-risk AI use case. EU AI Act risk tiers are correctly assigned to each use case. The sequencing logic is explicitly stated: it is clear why each initiative precedes or follows the others. |
| Merit | 8 to 10 | Most initiatives have all seven elements. One or two initiatives are missing one element, typically the dependency or the success metric. Data governance appears before AI deployment. Change management exists as a workstream but the budget estimate or owner function is absent or vague. The governance plan names an oversight body but the human override protocol lacks a specific trigger threshold. EU AI Act classification is present but one use case is misclassified or unclassified. |
| Pass | 5 to 7 | The roadmap exists and is structured by horizon. Several initiatives are missing two or more elements. Change management and governance are present but treated as sections rather than workstreams: they lack budget estimates, owner functions, or timelines. The sequencing has some logic but dependencies are not explicitly stated. Data governance appears in the roadmap but not necessarily before AI deployment. EU AI Act is mentioned but classification is incomplete or generic. |
| Fail | 0 to 4 | The roadmap is a list of initiatives without horizon tags, dependencies, owner functions, or success metrics. Change management appears as a bullet point in the risk register, not as a workstream. Governance is described as a principle rather than a structure: no oversight body is named, no review cadence is stated, no human override protocol exists. EU AI Act is absent or mentioned only in passing. |

**What separates Merit from Distinction:**
A Merit roadmap has the elements. A Distinction roadmap has the elements and the
sequencing logic: the reader understands not just what happens but why it happens
in that order and what breaks if the sequence is violated.

**The seven required elements per initiative:**

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Horizon tag | H1 (0 to 12 months), H2 (12 to 24 months), or H3 (24 months plus) |
| Owner function | Named role or team, not a person's name |
| Budget estimate | Order of magnitude in GEL, consistent with the ROI model |
| Timeline | Start month and end month within the 18-month window |
| Dependency | Named prerequisite initiative or capability |
| Success metric | One measurable metric achievable within the initiative timeline |
| Risk | Primary execution risk for this initiative |

## Criterion 3: Executive Communication and Storytelling
Weight: 25% | Points available: 10

This criterion assesses whether the Pyramid Principle is applied throughout the
presentation, whether every slide carries a conclusion headline rather than a topic
label, whether the narrative moves cleanly from problem to recommendation, and
whether the executive summary states conclusions rather than describing the
structure of the presentation.

| Level | Points | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| Distinction | 9 to 10 | Every slide headline states the conclusion of that slide. The executive summary states the three most important conclusions the board needs to act on, the recommendation in one sentence, and the ROI headline, before any analysis is shown. The narrative arc is clean: problem stated, evidence presented, options evaluated, recommendation made, implementation shown. The board could make a decision from the executive summary alone and the rest of the presentation provides the evidence. Transitions between sections are logical and explicit. The Q&A reveals that the team understands the analytical structure of the whole presentation, not just their individual sections. |
| Merit | 7 to 8 | Most slide headlines state conclusions. A small number remain as topic labels. The executive summary is mostly conclusions but one item describes the presentation structure rather than stating a finding. The narrative is clear and logical. The recommendation appears early in the presentation. Minor transitions are abrupt or missing. The team handles Q&A on their own sections well. |
| Pass | 5 to 6 | Some slide headlines state conclusions; the majority are topic labels. The executive summary is present but organised around sections of the presentation rather than conclusions. The recommendation appears eventually but is not the organising logic of the presentation. The narrative has some gaps. The team can answer questions about their own sections but struggles when Q&A crosses section boundaries. |
| Fail | 0 to 4 | Slide headlines are topic labels throughout. The executive summary describes what is coming rather than stating what the board should conclude. The recommendation appears at the end of the presentation rather than at the beginning. The narrative has no clear arc: evidence, options, and recommendations are not connected logically. The team presents their sections in isolation. |

**Topic label versus conclusion headline: the test**
A topic label names the subject. A conclusion headline states what the board should
conclude about that subject.

| Topic label (not acceptable) | Conclusion headline (required standard) |
|---|---|
| Industry Overview | Retail banking in Georgia faces structural disruption: three fintech entrants have captured 2.1 million users with 60 percent lower cost structures than incumbents |
| AI Use Cases | Two AI use cases deliver a combined GEL 22 million in annual cost reduction with a 14-month payback in the base case |
| Strategic Options | Option 2 is the only option that addresses both the cost structure gap and the customer experience deficit within 24 months |
| Risk Assessment | Three risks are material and all are manageable: data governance readiness, regulatory response time, and change management capacity |
| ROI Model | Base case: 18-month payback, 34 percent IRR, GEL 47 million NPV over 5 years; the downside scenario breaks even within 30 months |

## Criterion 4: Professional Presentation Standards and Polish
Weight: 15% | Points available: 6

This criterion assesses timing discipline, confidence and composure in delivery,
visual consistency and quality of the deck, equal participation of all team members,
and the ability to handle Q&A with precision and composure.

| Level | Points | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| Distinction | 6 | Presentation finishes within the 30-minute limit. All four team members present at least one full section with confidence and without reading from slides. The deck is visually consistent throughout with no placeholder text, no typos, and no formatting inconsistencies. Q&A is handled with composure: answers are direct, numbers are stated as numbers not narratives, and the team acknowledges the limits of their analysis honestly when appropriate. The team presents as a unit, not as individuals taking turns. |
| Merit | 4 to 5 | Presentation finishes close to the limit (within 2 minutes). Three of four members present with confidence. The deck is largely consistent with minor formatting issues. Q&A is handled well for core questions; one or two peripheral questions cause some hesitation. The team coordinates transitions smoothly. |
| Pass | 2 to 3 | Presentation finishes within 5 minutes of the limit or is stopped. Two or three members present; one member presents a disproportionately small section or reads from slides. The deck has some visual inconsistencies, placeholder text, or typos. Q&A produces some hesitation on numbers or assumptions. The team does not always present as a unit. |
| Fail | 0 to 1 | Presentation significantly exceeds or falls short of the time limit. One or two members carry the majority of the presentation. The deck has significant formatting issues, missing content, or placeholder text. Q&A produces contradiction between team members or an inability to defend basic claims. The team presents as individuals without coordination. |

## Instructor notes on applying this rubric

**Do not average across criteria before assigning a level.** Score each criterion
independently against its descriptor. A team that produces a Distinction-level
roadmap and a Fail-level executive summary does not get a Merit on both. They get
12 on Criterion 2 and the appropriate low score on Criterion 3.

**Peer scores are advisory, not determinative.** Peer scores are averaged with the
instructor score and count toward the final grade. If a peer score is an outlier
(more than 3 points above or below the instructor score on a criterion), note the
discrepancy and apply judgment. Do not allow peer scores to override a clear
assessment.

**Q&A performance is part of the assessment.** Criterion 1 and Criterion 4 are
both informed by Q&A. A team that presents well but cannot defend their numbers
or recommendation under Q&A cannot score Distinction on Criterion 1 regardless
of the quality of their slides. Note key Q&A moments when scoring.

**The final exam threshold is a hard rule.** A team that scores 19 out of 40 fails
the final exam threshold. This is a KIU and national framework requirement. There
is no rounding up across the threshold.
