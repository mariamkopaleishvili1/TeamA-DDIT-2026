## Deliverable 2: Comprehensive Final Report
Due: Sunday 7 June 2026 at 23:59

**What it is**
The written consulting report that synthesises all work from Weekends 1 through 4.
This is the permanent record of the team's analysis. It contains everything that
does not fit in slides: full methodology, detailed evidence, the complete financial
model with assumptions, and the governance framework in full.

The report submitted on 7 June must incorporate:
- all substantive feedback received during the Weekend 4 Saturday coaching session,
- all analytical gaps identified during the Sunday 31 May Q&A,
- the updated governance plan produced in the Saturday Responsible AI workshop,
- any corrections to the ROI model identified during the boardroom presentation.

**Submission file**
`final_report.pdf`

**Required sections**

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | The three most important conclusions. The recommendation in one sentence. The ROI headline. Written last, tested first. Maximum two pages. |
| Industry and competitive analysis | Rogers five domains with evidence. Christensen disruption classification with rationale. Porter Five Forces with named competitors and quantified forces where possible. Key competitive dynamics and their strategic consequences. |
| AI landscape and opportunity analysis | AI adoption evidence by use case. AI Opportunity Map with full scoring rationale. Strategic implications of AI trajectory. Gap analysis against the most AI-advanced comparable organisation. |
| Platform strategy analysis | Platform competitive structure. Network effect assessment. Client's current platform position. Platform threat assessment with named entrants. |
| Strategic options with full evaluation matrix | Three structurally distinct options with full descriptions. Four-criterion evaluation matrix with scores and rationale. Recommended option with three supporting reasons tied to specific findings. |
| Recommended business model | Value proposition in full. Operating model changes by function. Revenue model logic. Data and AI infrastructure requirements. |
| 18-month transformation roadmap | All initiatives with all seven elements per initiative. Horizon tags, owner functions, budget estimates, timelines, dependencies, success metrics, and risks. Sequencing rationale explicit for each initiative. |
| ROI model with full assumptions log | Investment breakdown by year. Benefit realisation by year. Base case, upside, and downside scenarios with differentiated assumptions. Payback period, IRR, and 5-year NPV. Sensitivity table for the two most consequential assumptions. All non-sourced numbers labelled as assumptions with a stated basis. |
| Risk register | All material risks with likelihood, impact, and mitigation. |
| Change management plan | Change management as a named workstream. Budget at 20 to 30 percent of total transformation cost, stated as a GEL figure. Owner function. Timeline aligned to the roadmap. Key activities by phase: stakeholder analysis, communication plan, training and capability building, adoption measurement. |
| Governance plan | Oversight body: name, membership by function, decision rights, review cadence. Human override protocol per high-risk AI use case: specific trigger threshold, named override authority by role, documentation and audit requirement, maximum review time before default action. EU AI Act risk tier classification for each AI use case with rationale. Georgian data protection law obligations for use cases involving individual automated decisions. Escalation path when the oversight body cannot reach a decision. |
| Responsible AI reflection | One section added as a result of Weekend 4 Saturday content. For each AI use case deployed in H1: state the bias or fairness risk, the transparency obligation to affected individuals, and the accountability structure if the model produces an adverse outcome. This section does not exist in the midterm. It is a Weekend 4 addition. |
| AI tool disclosure appendix | If AI tools were used substantially in research or writing, disclose which tools, for which sections, and how outputs were verified. Required per syllabus AI tool policy. |

**Length**
No minimum. No maximum. The report should be as long as the analysis requires
and no longer. Padding is penalised. Incompleteness is penalised more.

## Deliverable 3: 18-Month Transformation Roadmap
Due: Sunday 7 June 2026 at 23:59

**What it is**
The complete roadmap as a standalone document. Also included as a section of
the final report but submitted separately so it can be read and assessed
independently.

**Submission file**
`transformation_roadmap_final.[pdf or xlsx]`

**Required elements per initiative**

Every initiative must contain all seven of the following. An initiative missing
any element is incomplete.

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Horizon tag | H1 (0 to 12 months), H2 (12 to 24 months), or H3 (24 months plus) |
| Owner function | Named role or team. Not a person's name. |
| Budget estimate | Order of magnitude in GEL, consistent with the ROI model. |
| Timeline | Start month and end month within the 18-month window. |
| Dependency | Named prerequisite initiative or capability. |
| Success metric | One measurable metric achievable within the initiative timeline. |
| Risk | Primary execution risk for this initiative. |

**Sequencing rules**
- Data governance framework: months 1 to 3. No exceptions.
- Oversight body establishment: months 2 to 4.
- Human override protocols documented and tested: before any high-risk AI use case go-live.
- Change management workstream: runs in parallel with all H1 and H2 initiatives.
- First quarterly governance review: month 6.

**Post-presentation update**
If the Sunday 31 May Q&A identified weaknesses in the roadmap sequencing,
dependency logic, or initiative scope, the 7 June version must address them.
State in a one-paragraph note at the start of the document what was changed
and why.

## Deliverable 4: Change Management and Governance Plan
Due: Sunday 7 June 2026 at 23:59

**What it is**
The governance and change management framework as a standalone document. Also
included as a section of the final report but submitted separately.

**Submission file**
`governance_change_management_plan.pdf`

This document must be updated to reflect the governance plan produced during
the Weekend 4 Saturday Responsible AI workshop. The governance plan in this
document supersedes any earlier draft.

**Change management section must include**
- Change management defined as a named workstream, not a risk mitigation.
- Budget: 20 to 30 percent of total transformation cost. Stated as a GEL figure.
- Owner function: the role or team responsible for leading change management.
- Timeline: a phased activity plan aligned to the roadmap.
- Key activities: stakeholder analysis, communication plan, training and capability building, adoption measurement.

**Governance section must include**
- Oversight body: name, membership by function, decision rights, review cadence.
- Human override protocol for every high-risk AI use case:
  specific trigger threshold, named override authority by role,
  documentation and audit requirement, maximum review time.
- EU AI Act risk tier classification for each AI use case with rationale.
- Georgian data protection law obligations for use cases involving individual automated decisions.
- Escalation path when the oversight body cannot reach a decision.
- Responsible AI principles applied to each H1 AI use case: bias and fairness risk, transparency obligation, accountability structure for adverse outcomes.

## Deliverable 5: Responsible AI Assessment
Due: Sunday 7 June 2026 at 23:59

**What it is**
A new standalone document produced as a direct result of Weekend 4 Saturday
Block 1 content. It does not exist in the midterm. It is required for the final.

**Submission file**
`responsible_ai_assessment.pdf`

This document applies the EU AI Act framework and responsible AI principles
directly to the client's AI use case portfolio. It extends the governance plan
with the ethical and regulatory analysis developed on Saturday 30 May.

**Required content**

For each AI use case in the roadmap:

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| EU AI Act risk tier | Unacceptable, high, limited, or minimal. With rationale for the classification. |
| Bias and fairness risk | What demographic, geographic, or behavioural bias could this model produce? What is the mitigation? |
| Transparency obligation | What must the organisation disclose to individuals affected by this model's decisions, and when? |
| Accountability structure | Who is accountable if this model produces an adverse outcome? What is the escalation path? |
| Georgian law obligation | What specific obligation under the Law of Georgia on Personal Data Protection applies to this use case? |
| Human override threshold | The specific condition that triggers human review. Not a narrative. A measurable threshold. |

**Why this deliverable exists**
The Weekend 4 Saturday session introduced responsible AI principles, EU AI Act
risk classification, and governance design as the final analytical layer of the
course. The responsible AI assessment captures that learning as a consulting
output. It reflects the full arc of the semester: from disruption analysis in
Weekend 1 to ethical deployment governance in Weekend 4.

## Assessment linkage

All five deliverables are assessed together as the 40-point final exam.

| Criterion | Weight | Primary instrument |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic insight and analytical rigor | 30% | Final report and responsible AI assessment |
| Quality and feasibility of transformation roadmap | 30% | Roadmap and governance plan |
| Executive communication and storytelling | 25% | Presentation deck |
| Professional presentation standards and polish | 15% | Live presentation on Sunday 31 May |

A strong presentation with weak documentation loses points on strategic insight
and analytical rigor. Strong documentation with a weak presentation loses points
on executive communication and professional standards. Both must meet the standard.

The one-week gap between the presentation and the documentation deadline is your
opportunity. Use Sunday evening and the following week to close every gap identified
in the boardroom. The 7 June submission is the version that counts.
