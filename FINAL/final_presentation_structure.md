## The standard

This is a boardroom-quality consulting engagement, not a student presentation. The
board has limited time and high stakes. Every slide earns its place. Every minute
is accounted for. Every claim is defensible under Q&A.

Two rules that override everything else:

**Rule 1: Recommendation first.**
The executive summary is slide 1. The board sees the conclusion before the evidence.
A presentation that builds to the recommendation at the end fails the Pyramid
Principle and loses marks on executive communication.

**Rule 2: Conclusion headlines on every slide.**
Every slide headline states the conclusion of that slide, not the topic.
"AI Use Cases" is a topic label. It is not acceptable.
"Two AI use cases deliver GEL 22M in annual cost reduction with a 14-month payback"
is a conclusion headline. It is the standard.

## Slide structure

Total slides: 30 minimum. Hard upper limit: 35.
Appendix slides do not count toward the limit and are not presented. They are for Q&A backup only.

| # | Section | Slides | What it must contain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Executive summary | 1 to 2 | The three most important conclusions from the entire project stated as conclusions, not topics. The recommendation in one sentence. The ROI headline: payback period, IRR, and NPV in the base case. Written last but presented first. |
| 2 | Industry and competitive context | 3 to 4 | Rogers five domains applied to the specific industry. Christensen classification of the primary disruption force. Porter Five Forces summary with named competitors. Key competitive dynamics and their strategic consequence. Every finding followed by its strategic implication: "This means that..." |
| 3 | AI landscape and opportunity | 3 to 4 | Current AI adoption state in the industry with evidence. Top three AI use cases from the AI Opportunity Map with scoring. Strategic implications of the AI capability trajectory for the client. Named benchmark: what is the most AI-advanced comparable organisation doing, and what gap does that represent? |
| 4 | Platform dynamics | 2 to 3 | Platform competitive structure in the industry. Client's current position in the platform landscape. Threat assessment from platform entrants: which platform player poses the greatest structural threat and why. |
| 5 | Strategic options evaluation | 4 to 5 | Three structurally distinct options. Distinct means different value proposition, different revenue model, and different capability requirement. Not three intensities of the same direction. Four-criterion evaluation matrix with scores. Recommended option stated with three supporting reasons, each tied to a specific finding from the analysis. |
| 6 | Business model detail | 3 to 4 | The recommended business model in full. Value proposition: who is the customer, what problem is solved, why is the solution better than alternatives. Operating model changes: which processes change, which roles change, which capabilities must be built. Revenue model: how does the organisation earn money differently from today. Data and AI infrastructure requirements: what must be in place before the business model works. |
| 7 | 18-month transformation roadmap | 3 to 4 | Three Horizons portfolio showing all initiatives by horizon. Milestone map for the full 18-month window. Every initiative must include all seven elements: horizon tag, owner function, budget estimate, timeline, dependency, success metric, and risk. Data governance must appear in months 1 to 3 before any AI deployment initiative. Change management must appear as a named workstream with a budget estimate. |
| 8 | ROI and financial case | 3 to 4 | Base case financials: total investment, annual benefit, payback period, IRR, 5-year NPV. Three scenario analysis: base case, upside, and downside. The downside must be the worst credible scenario, not a mild variation. All assumptions stated explicitly. Year 1 capital requirement broken into capital expenditure and operating expenditure. Sensitivity table showing payback under the two most consequential assumption changes. |
| 9 | Risk, change management, and governance | 2 to 3 | Top three risks with named mitigations. Change management as a standalone workstream: budget at 20 to 30 percent of total transformation cost, named owner function, timeline. Governance plan: oversight body name and membership, decision rights, review cadence, human override protocol for the two highest-risk AI use cases. EU AI Act risk classification for each AI use case. |
| 10 | Implementation logic and next steps | 1 to 2 | Who owns what: named owner function per major workstream. The three decisions the board must make today to unblock the plan. Key dependencies that determine whether the 18-month timeline is achievable. What happens if the board delays by six months: cost of inaction stated as a number. |

## Assessment criteria

| Criterion | Weight | What is being assessed |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic insight and analytical rigor | 30% | Are conclusions specific and defensible? Does every technology finding have a strategic implication? Is the recommendation supported by evidence from the analysis? Can the team defend numbers under Q&A? |
| Quality and feasibility of transformation roadmap | 30% | Does every initiative have all seven required elements? Is sequencing credible and dependency-aware? Does data governance precede AI deployment? Is change management budgeted and owned? |
| Executive communication and storytelling | 25% | Is the Pyramid Principle applied throughout? Does every slide have a conclusion headline? Does the narrative move cleanly from problem to recommendation without detours? Is the executive summary three conclusions, not three topics? |
| Professional presentation standards and polish | 15% | Is timing controlled? Do all four members present with confidence? Is the deck visually consistent with no placeholder text or typos? Is Q&A handled with composure and precision? |

## Common failures

These are the most consistent weaknesses from the Weekend 3 midterm. Do not repeat them.

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Executive summary describes the presentation structure instead of stating conclusions | Write three conclusion sentences. If you cannot, the analysis is not complete. |
| Strategic options are differentiated by budget or scale, not by value proposition | Apply the structural distinction test: different value proposition, different revenue model, different capability requirement. |
| ROI sensitivity table shows base case and upside only | The board constructs the downside in Q&A. Show it first. Name the two worst credible assumption failures. |
| Data governance does not appear before AI deployment in the roadmap | Move data governance to months 1 to 3. No AI deployment initiative can precede it. |
| Change management appears as a bullet in the risk register | Move it to the roadmap as a named workstream with a budget and an owner function. |
| Governance plan states principles without naming the oversight body or human override threshold | Name the body. Define its membership and decision rights. State the specific trigger threshold for human review. |
