# Transformation Failure Patterns v1

## Project

Team A - Georgian Prediction-Market Platform  
Industry: Fintech  
Market: Republic of Georgia  
Recommended option: Regulatory Sandbox Pathway with responsible-use controls embedded from day one

---

## Purpose

This document applies transformation-failure patterns to the recommended strategy for launching a Georgian prediction-market platform.

The goal is to prevent the project from becoming a technically interesting but strategically unexecutable initiative.

The key lesson from the framework is simple:

> Transformation failure is usually not caused by lack of technology. It is caused by business-model protection, organisational resistance, poor sequencing, weak governance, and failure to fund new models separately from the core business.

For this project, the same logic applies. A Georgian prediction-market platform will not fail because the app cannot be built. It will fail if the operating model, regulatory path, liquidity design, and bank incentives are not aligned before launch.

---

# 1. Strategic Failure Risk

## Core transformation risk

The recommended platform is not just a new fintech feature. It is a new regulated marketplace category.

This creates a transformation risk for a potential bank sponsor such as TBC:

- the product may look too close to gambling,
- the unit economics may be weak at micro-stakes scale,
- compliance requirements may be higher than expected,
- liquidity may require recurring subsidy,
- the initiative may conflict with the bank’s trusted brand,
- and the bank may judge the risk too high relative to existing brokerage or deposit products.

The main risk is therefore not technical feasibility. It is whether the bank can approve, fund, and govern a product that does not fit neatly inside the existing banking or brokerage operating model.

# 2. Blockbuster Pattern: Protecting Legacy Revenue

## Pattern summary

Blockbuster failed partly because late-fee revenue was too important to give up. The business knew the model was changing, but the short-term P&L made the correct long-term move difficult to approve.

The framework’s lesson is:

> Every organisation has its equivalent of late-fee revenue. The board-level constraint must be named explicitly.

## Equivalent risk in this project

For a Georgian bank-backed prediction-market platform, the equivalent of “late-fee revenue” is not one single revenue line. It is the bank’s broader low-risk trust model:

- deposit gathering,
- traditional brokerage,
- wealth-management credibility,
- payment relationships,
- conservative regulatory reputation,
- and the bank’s image as a safe financial institution.

A prediction-market platform could threaten this trust if users, regulators, or media perceive it as gambling in financial language.

## Why this matters

A bank may rationally hesitate to support prediction markets even if the product has growth potential.

The board may ask:

- Will this damage the bank’s reputation?
- Will NBG view this as gambling?
- Will payment partners accept it?
- Will this cannibalize brokerage growth?
- Will it attract speculative users the bank does not want?
- Will the compliance cost exceed the upside?

This is the project’s Blockbuster problem.

The existing model is safer in the short term. The new model is more uncertain, more visible, and more reputationally sensitive.

## Required mitigation

The recommendation must not be framed as:

> “Launch a prediction-market app.”

It should be framed as:

> “Create a controlled, sandbox-tested event-contract marketplace with bank-grade controls, responsible-use protections, and limited initial markets.”

## Board-level framing

The board should not be asked to choose between comfort and change. It should be asked to choose between two risks:

| Option | Risk |
|---|---|
| Do nothing | Lose future retail speculation and event-based engagement to offshore crypto, betting, or global prediction platforms |
| Act now | Take controlled regulatory, liquidity, and reputational risk through a sandbox-first pilot |

## Implication for final recommendation

The platform should only proceed if the bank can protect its trusted brand through:

- NBG pre-consultation,
- clear separation from gambling,
- conservative event taxonomy,
- responsible-use limits,
- full KYC / AML,
- transparent resolution rules,
- and human governance over AI-supported decisions.

# 3. Kodak Pattern: Technology Without Commercial Operating Model

## Pattern summary

Kodak invented the digital camera but failed to commercialise the digital model. The problem was not invention. The problem was operating model, distribution, incentives, and failure to separate the new business from the old one.

The framework’s lesson is:

> R&D without an operating model to commercialise the invention is not a competitive asset.

## Equivalent risk in this project

A Georgian prediction-market platform could make the same mistake.

The team may be able to design:

- a user-friendly yes/no contract interface,
- AI-generated market descriptions,
- automated event monitoring,
- probability pricing models,
- and a modern digital product.

But that is not enough.

The platform also needs:

- regulatory classification,
- payment rails,
- market-maker support,
- official event-data sources,
- responsible-use controls,
- dispute handling,
- compliance reporting,
- and regulator-ready audit trails.

Without these, the product remains a demo, not an operating business.

## What must be commercialised

The commercial product is not only the app.

The commercial product is a full operating system for event markets:

```text
Event design
→ legal review
→ source validation
→ liquidity support
→ controlled listing
→ user protection
→ trading surveillance
→ outcome resolution
→ dispute handling
→ regulator reporting
```

## Required mitigation

The project must include a target operating model before public launch.

At minimum, this requires:

- Market Governance Committee,
- event taxonomy,
- official source registry,
- market approval workflow,
- AI risk scoring with human oversight,
- liquidity monitoring,
- responsible-use rules,
- and resolution / dispute procedures.

## Implication for final recommendation

The platform should not launch as a general marketplace. It should launch first as a controlled pilot with a limited number of verifiable markets.

Recommended first market types:

- GEL/USD exchange-rate bands,
- NBG policy-rate decisions,
- inflation releases,
- public macroeconomic indicators,
- election turnout,
- clearly verifiable civic outcomes.

Avoid in the first phase:

- sports markets,
- celebrity markets,
- criminal cases,
- war outcomes,
- medical outcomes,
- markets based on private individuals,
- ambiguous political claims,
- events without official data sources.

# 4. Failure Pattern 1: Technology Pilot Never Scales to Production

## Pattern summary

A technology pilot fails to scale when data quality, architecture, governance, or change-management prerequisites are not completed before deployment.

## Risk in this project

The prediction-market platform is especially exposed to this pattern.

A pilot may work with:

- a small user group,
- manual market approval,
- manual outcome resolution,
- manual compliance review,
- and one or two simple markets.

But this does not mean it can scale.

Scaling requires infrastructure that the pilot may not prove:

- automated event-source ingestion,
- live order-book monitoring,
- liquidity analytics,
- market-integrity alerts,
- user risk scoring,
- complaint and dispute analytics,
- regulator-ready reporting,
- and payment / KYC integration.

## Most vulnerable initiative

The most vulnerable roadmap initiative is:

> AI-assisted market lifecycle management.

This includes AI screening of events, market surveillance, responsible-use scoring, and outcome-resolution support.

## Why it may fail

It may fail if the platform tries to deploy AI before building:

- clean event-source registry,
- approved event taxonomy,
- audit logs,
- user-behavior dataset,
- order-book data structure,
- market-maker performance data,
- responsible-use dataset,
- and escalation rules.

## Required sequencing

| Prerequisite | Must happen before | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| Approved event taxonomy | AI event screening | Market Governance Committee |
| Official event-source registry | AI outcome-resolution support | Data Governance Lead |
| Market approval workflow | AI-generated market descriptions | Market Operations |
| Order-book data pipeline | Liquidity and manipulation alerts | Data / AI Team |
| Responsible-use rules | User risk scoring | Responsible Use Lead |
| Audit log design | Regulator-ready reporting | Compliance / Data Team |

## Required mitigation

Do not present AI deployment as a month-4 full operating capability.

Instead:

- H1 should use AI only for decision support.
- Humans should approve every listing and payout during sandbox.
- H2 should automate monitoring and reporting after pilot data exists.

## Board message

The pilot should not be judged only by user signups. It should be judged by whether it produces the data and controls needed to scale.

# 5. Failure Pattern 2: Protecting Legacy Revenue Prevents Investment

## Pattern summary

The board approves only initiatives that do not threaten existing revenue, brand, or operating logic. The transformation therefore never gets funded properly.

## Risk in this project

A bank may view the prediction-market platform as risky because it does not fit the existing business model.

Traditional bank and brokerage value comes from:

- trust,
- deposits,
- wealth management,
- lending,
- payments,
- brokerage access,
- conservative regulation,
- and long-term customer relationships.

Prediction markets introduce:

- short-term speculation,
- politically sensitive events,
- possible gambling comparison,
- user harm concerns,
- liquidity cost,
- and market-manipulation risk.

## Client’s equivalent of Blockbuster’s late-fee revenue

For this project, the equivalent is:

> the bank’s trust-based, low-risk financial-services franchise.

The bank may reject the platform not because it lacks potential, but because it threatens the brand logic that makes the bank valuable.

## Cost of inaction

The cost of doing nothing is that digitally active speculative users may move toward:

- offshore crypto platforms,
- betting apps,
- global prediction markets,
- informal Telegram / OTC speculation,
- or foreign platforms outside Georgian supervision.

This weakens the bank’s ability to capture future retail financial behavior.

## Required mitigation

The financial case should not pretend the platform will be profitable immediately.

Instead, it should show:

- pilot economics are likely loss-making,
- base-case breakeven requires meaningful scale,
- bank backing reduces CAC and payment cost,
- early losses buy regulatory learning and market-position optionality,
- and the cost of inaction is loss of future retail engagement.

## Board message

The board should fund the platform as a ring-fenced strategic option, not as a normal short-payback bank product.

# 6. Failure Pattern 3: New Initiative Run Inside the Existing P&L

## Pattern summary

New initiatives fail when they are forced to meet the same ROI hurdle, risk appetite, and payback timeline as the core business.

## Risk in this project

A prediction-market platform will not look attractive if it is judged like an existing bank product.

In the early phase, the platform requires:

- legal work,
- compliance setup,
- NBG engagement,
- product build,
- liquidity subsidy,
- market-maker support,
- user education,
- and responsible-use infrastructure.

Revenue arrives later and depends on trading scale.

Therefore, if the project is evaluated using a normal bank-product hurdle rate or short payback rule, it may be killed before it has a chance to learn.

## Why the existing P&L is wrong

The platform is closer to a Horizon 2 / Horizon 3 initiative than an H1 efficiency project.

It is not mainly about near-term profit. It is about:

- category creation,
- regulatory learning,
- customer-behavior data,
- future trading engagement,
- and first-mover advantage in regulated event markets.

## Required mitigation

Create a ring-fenced innovation budget.

Recommended structure:

| Budget category | Purpose | Expected return timeline |
|---|---|---|
| H1 operating budget | Governance, legal, initial product design, compliance setup | 0 to 6 months; no direct profit expectation |
| H2 sandbox budget | Pilot, liquidity support, user testing, market surveillance, NBG reporting | 6 to 18 months; learning and validation |
| H3 option budget | Full public launch, broader market expansion, advanced AI / data infrastructure | 18+ months; scale economics |

## Decision rule

The platform should not be judged only on Year 1 profit.

It should be judged on:

- NBG comfort,
- payment-partner acceptance,
- active-user retention,
- dispute rate,
- liquidity quality,
- user harm indicators,
- CAC,
- contribution per active user,
- and breakeven path.

## Board message

A prediction-market platform should be funded like a strategic option with milestone gates, not like a mature banking product.

# 7. Failure Pattern 4: Roadmap Is a List With Dates, Not a Plan

## Pattern summary

A roadmap fails when it lists initiatives and dates but does not show dependencies, owners, budget estimates, or success metrics.

## Risk in this project

A weak roadmap might say:

- Month 1: NBG engagement
- Month 3: MVP
- Month 6: Pilot
- Month 12: Launch

This is not enough.

It does not explain:

- who owns each step,
- what must happen first,
- how much it costs,
- what success means,
- what blocks the next phase,
- and which decisions require board approval.

## Required roadmap elements

Every roadmap initiative should include seven elements:

1. name,
2. horizon tag,
3. timeline,
4. owner function,
5. budget estimate,
6. dependency on prior initiative,
7. measurable success metric.

## Correct roadmap structure

| Initiative | Horizon | Timeline | Owner | Budget order of magnitude | Dependency | Success metric |
|---|---|---:|---|---:|---|---|
| NBG pre-consultation package | H1 | Months 1-2 | Regulatory & Governance | GEL 50k-100k | Board approval to engage | Written feedback or documented meeting outcome |
| Event taxonomy and prohibited-market list | H1 | Months 1-2 | Market Governance Committee | GEL 20k-40k | Legal counsel retained | 100% of proposed markets classified before review |
| Official event-source registry | H1 | Months 2-3 | Data Governance | GEL 20k-50k | Event taxonomy | Source-confidence score above 95% for listed markets |
| Market contract templates | H1 | Months 2-4 | Market Operations | GEL 20k-40k | Event-source registry | 90%+ of markets use standard approved template |
| Responsible-use framework | H1 | Months 3-4 | Responsible Use Lead | GEL 30k-60k | Legal / compliance review | Loss limits and cooling-off rules approved before pilot |
| MVP trading interface | H1 | Months 3-6 | Product & Engineering | GEL 300k-600k | Market templates and KYC design | Internal test markets execute without settlement error |
| KYC / GEL payment integration | H1 | Months 4-6 | Payments & KYC | GEL 100k-250k | Bank / PSP partner selected | 95%+ successful verified onboarding in test cohort |
| Controlled sandbox pilot | H2 | Months 7-12 | General Manager | GEL 500k-1.5M | NBG comfort, MVP, KYC, responsible-use controls | Pilot dispute rate below 1% of active market traders |
| Liquidity and market-maker program | H2 | Months 7-12 | Market Operations & Liquidity | GEL 300k-600k | Market-maker agreement | Top markets maintain spread below 8% |
| AI market-surveillance dashboard | H2 | Months 8-12 | Data & AI | GEL 150k-300k | Order-book data pipeline | 100% of abnormal price moves reviewed within SLA |
| Regulator-ready reporting automation | H2 | Months 10-14 | Regulatory & Data Teams | GEL 100k-250k | Pilot audit logs | Monthly NBG / partner reports produced on time |
| Public launch decision | H2 | Months 15-18 | Board / Executive Steering Committee | n/a | Pilot results and legal classification | Go / no-go decision based on regulatory, liquidity, and unit-economics gates |

## Board message

The roadmap must be presented as a sequenced investment plan, not a project calendar.

# 8. Application to Weekend 3 Questions

## Question 1: Which roadmap initiative is most at risk of Pattern 1?

The most at-risk initiative is:

> AI-assisted market surveillance and event-risk scoring.

Reason:

The AI layer depends on clean order-book data, approved event categories, source registry, account-linkage data, responsible-use rules, and audit logs. If these prerequisites are missing, the AI tool will create alerts but not reliable operational decisions.

## Question 2: What is the client’s equivalent of Blockbuster’s late-fee revenue?

The equivalent is:

> the bank’s trust-based, low-risk franchise in deposits, payments, brokerage, and wealth management.

The prediction-market product could create reputational risk if it is perceived as gambling. Therefore, the initiative threatens the bank’s brand logic even if the technology works.

## Question 3: Does the financial model use the same hurdle rate for H1 and H3 initiatives?

It should not.

The correct structure is:

- H1 initiatives should be judged on readiness, control, and cost discipline.
- H2 sandbox initiatives should be judged on learning, adoption, regulatory comfort, and liquidity quality.
- H3 scale initiatives should be judged on breakeven path, active traders, contribution margin, and defensibility.

A single hurdle rate would make the project look unattractive too early.

## Question 4: Does every roadmap initiative include the seven required elements?

The final roadmap should include:

- name,
- horizon tag,
- timeline,
- owner function,
- budget estimate,
- dependency,
- measurable success metric.

Any initiative without these seven elements should be treated as incomplete.

# 9. Executive Summary Insert

The following paragraph can be added to the final report or presentation executive summary:

> The main transformation risk is not whether a prediction-market app can be built. It is whether a Georgian bank can approve and govern a product that sits between finance, gambling, and virtual assets without damaging regulatory trust. The client’s equivalent of Blockbuster’s late-fee revenue is its trust-based banking franchise: deposits, payments, brokerage, and conservative regulatory reputation. Therefore, the recommended model should be funded as a ring-fenced, sandbox-first strategic option, not as a normal short-payback banking product. The platform should proceed only if NBG engagement, bank-backed distribution, responsible-use controls, and liquidity support are solved together.
