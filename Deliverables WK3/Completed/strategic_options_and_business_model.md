# Strategic Options and Recommended Business Model

## Project

Team A: Georgian Prediction-Market Platform
Industry: Fintech
Market: Republic of Georgia
Client assumption: TBC-backed launch model
Recommended strategic option: TBC-owned, GEL-settled prediction-market platform under an NBG special-designation or sandbox-first route

---

## Purpose

This document combines two final-report deliverables:

1. Strategic options with full evaluation matrix.
2. Recommended business model.

The analysis reflects the final version of the project story. The proposal is no longer framed as a standalone fintech app. The recommended strategy is a TBC-owned wallet-to-market platform integrated into or adjacent to the mobile banking ecosystem.

The core strategic question is:

> Should TBC own the Georgian event-contract category, or allow foreign platforms, crypto exchanges, betting operators, or competing banks to capture the wallet, customer relationship, and market infrastructure first?

The recommendation is:

> TBC should approve an 18-month controlled launch path for a TBC-owned, GEL-settled prediction-market platform under an NBG special-designation or sandbox-first route, with mobile banking integration, responsible-use controls, official-source resolution, and regulator-ready governance built before public scale.

# Part 1. Strategic Options Evaluation

## 1. Strategic options overview

The three options are structurally distinct. They are not three versions of the same plan at different budget levels. Each option has a different value proposition, revenue model, ownership logic, and capability requirement.

| Option   | Strategic direction                   | Core idea                                                                                                        |
| -------- | ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Option 1 | TBC-owned GEL wallet platform         | TBC owns the wallet, mobile banking access, customer relationship, GEL settlement, and governance model.         |
| Option 2 | International platform partnership    | TBC partners with or white-labels a global prediction-market player such as Polymarket or Kalshi-style provider. |
| Option 3 | Crypto/VASP-first standalone platform | Launch through crypto rails, USDC-style settlement, or VASP infrastructure, with weaker bank integration.        |

## 2. Option 1: TBC-owned GEL wallet platform

### Description

Option 1 is a TBC-owned, GEL-settled prediction-market platform launched through an NBG special-designation or sandbox-first route.

The product would be integrated into or adjacent to TBC’s mobile banking app. Users would access event contracts through a TBC-linked wallet or platform balance. TBC would control KYC, wallet funding, settlement, withdrawal flow, responsible-use rules, market approval, data governance, support escalation, and regulator-facing reporting.

The product would begin with narrow official-source markets, such as:

* inflation releases;
* GEL exchange-rate bands;
* NBG policy decisions;
* election turnout;
* official macroeconomic or civic data releases.

### Value proposition

For users, the value is simple public-event exposure inside a trusted bank-linked environment.

For TBC, the value is wallet ownership, retained balances, customer engagement, market intelligence, trading-related fee revenue, and first-mover position in regulated Georgian event contracts.

For regulators, the value is a controlled market structure with KYC, AML, official-source resolution, exposure limits, responsible-use controls, and auditability.

### Revenue model

The revenue model is based primarily on trading turnover.

The base-case formula is:

```text
Active traders × monthly turnover per active user × take rate = monthly gross revenue
```

In the updated ROI model:

```text
65,100 active users × GEL 6,546 monthly turnover × 1% take rate = GEL 4.26M monthly gross revenue
```

The main revenue stream is spread capture or maker/taker trading fee. Future optional revenue streams may include compliant market-creation fees, analytics products, premium features, or B2B/institutional products, but these should not be treated as base-case revenue before liquidity is proven.

### Capability requirement

This option requires the highest internal capability build, but it also creates the strongest strategic control.

Required capabilities include:

* NBG engagement and legal classification;
* TBC-linked GEL wallet design;
* mobile banking app integration;
* market taxonomy and approval workflow;
* official source registry;
* liquidity and market-maker programme;
* market surveillance;
* responsible-use controls;
* customer support and dispute handling;
* data and AI governance;
* regulator and payment-partner reporting.

### Main risk

The main risk is execution complexity. TBC must build a supervised marketplace, not only a trading screen. The platform must prove regulatory comfort, liquidity quality, user understanding, responsible-use performance, and unit economics before public scale.

### Strategic interpretation

This is the most defensible option because it keeps the most important value layers inside TBC: wallet, user relationship, mobile app access, settlement, governance, data, and trust.

## 3. Option 2: International platform partnership

### Description

Option 2 is a partnership or white-label route with a global prediction-market platform or technology provider. Polymarket, Kalshi, or similar global players would be used as benchmarks for market structure, interface design, liquidity, or exchange operations.

This option could reduce product-development time and provide access to existing knowledge about prediction-market design. It may also support faster learning around market pages, pricing interfaces, liquidity design, and user engagement.

### Value proposition

For users, the value is faster access to a more mature prediction-market interface.

For TBC, the value is speed to market and reduced technical uncertainty.

For the international partner, the value is access to Georgian users, TBC distribution, and local payment rails.

### Revenue model

The revenue model would likely involve one or more of the following:

* revenue share;
* licensing fee;
* white-label platform fee;
* payment-processing margin;
* wallet funding margin;
* limited spread or transaction-fee share.

The problem is that platform economics may be split with the external provider. If the partner controls the trading platform, market data, wallet, or user experience, TBC may capture less value than in Option 1.

### Capability requirement

This option requires:

* partner selection;
* legal review of responsibility allocation;
* data-sharing and customer-ownership agreement;
* Georgian regulatory classification;
* TBC wallet or payment integration;
* local KYC and AML adaptation;
* Georgian-language support and risk disclosure;
* local official-source market design;
* joint incident and dispute protocols.

### Main risk

The main risk is strategic dependency.

A simple partnership does not solve the hardest Georgian problems:

* NBG classification;
* GEL settlement;
* local consumer protection;
* TBC wallet ownership;
* responsible-use expectations;
* bank-brand risk;
* regulator-facing governance.

It may also cause TBC to lose the most valuable parts of the platform: the customer relationship, product data, market design, and platform economics.

### Strategic interpretation

This option is useful as a support route, but weak as the main strategy. TBC can partner for know-how, liquidity, or technology support, but should not outsource ownership of the Georgian wallet and governance layer.

## 4. Option 3: Crypto/VASP-first standalone platform

### Description

Option 3 is a crypto-native or VASP-first launch route. The platform would use crypto rails, USDC-style settlement, VASP infrastructure, or a standalone fintech architecture rather than a TBC-owned GEL wallet as the core.

This route may be faster if banking integration and NBG special designation are slow. It may also appeal to crypto users who are already familiar with wallets, volatility, trading, and tokenized settlement.

### Value proposition

For users, the value is flexible, fast, crypto-native event trading.

For the platform operator, the value is lower dependence on bank systems and possibly faster technical deployment.

For crypto-native users, the product may feel closer to existing trading behavior.

### Revenue model

The revenue model would be similar to global crypto-native prediction-market models:

* spread capture;
* trading fees;
* liquidity incentives;
* crypto wallet flows;
* possible tokenized settlement or stablecoin-based payment flows.

However, the mainstream Georgian market may be harder to reach because many users still require GEL access, bank trust, and local payment clarity.

### Capability requirement

This option requires:

* VASP registration or partnership;
* wallet infrastructure;
* crypto custody or non-custodial wallet design;
* blockchain or stablecoin settlement logic;
* AML and Travel Rule compliance;
* user education around crypto settlement;
* conversion between GEL and crypto assets;
* legal review of whether VASP status solves or does not solve event-contract classification.

### Main risk

The main risk is regulatory and mainstream trust weakness.

A crypto-first route may not solve the central legal question: whether event contracts are permitted and how they should be classified. It may also make the platform look more speculative, less bank-grade, and less suitable for mass-market adoption.

### Strategic interpretation

This option should remain a fallback or niche route, not the recommended base case. It may support a narrower crypto-native pilot if the GEL-settled path is blocked, but it does not create the strongest TBC strategic position.

# 5. Four-Criterion Evaluation Matrix

## 5.1 Scoring method

Each option is scored from 1 to 5 on four criteria.

| Score | Meaning     |
| ----: | ----------- |
|     5 | Very strong |
|     4 | Strong      |
|     3 | Moderate    |
|     2 | Weak        |
|     1 | Very weak   |

The four criteria are:

1. **Strategic control**
   Does the option allow TBC to own the wallet, customer relationship, data, settlement flow, and platform economics?

2. **Regulatory and trust fit**
   Does the option support NBG engagement, responsible-use controls, bank-grade trust, and clear separation from betting-style positioning?

3. **Financial attractiveness**
   Does the option support the updated ROI logic through adoption, turnover velocity, low payment cost, liquidity quality, and margin capture?

4. **Execution feasibility**
   Can the option be implemented within the 18-month roadmap with credible capabilities, governance, and dependencies?

## 5.3 Scoring rationale

### Option 1: TBC-owned GEL wallet platform

**Strategic control: 5 / 5**
TBC keeps control over wallet funding, KYC, mobile banking access, GEL settlement, user relationship, data, support, governance, and regulator-facing evidence.

**Regulatory and trust fit: 5 / 5**
This option is best aligned with an NBG special-designation or sandbox-first route. It allows the product to be framed as a supervised event-contract marketplace rather than a betting-style or crypto-native product.

**Financial attractiveness: 5 / 5**
This option best supports the ROI model because bank distribution can lower CAC, wallet ownership can reduce payment friction, and TBC trust can support adoption. The base case assumes 65,100 active users, GEL 426.1M monthly platform turnover, and GEL 4.26M monthly gross revenue.

**Execution feasibility: 4 / 5**
Execution is complex because TBC must build a supervised marketplace. However, the 18-month roadmap breaks the work into staged governance, NBG engagement, source registry, wallet design, MVP workflow, sandbox pilot, liquidity support, monitoring, and launch-gate decision.

### Option 2: International platform partnership

**Strategic control: 3 / 5**
TBC may gain know-how, but risks losing control of product experience, user data, market design, wallet behavior, and platform economics.

**Regulatory and trust fit: 3 / 5**
A global partner does not automatically solve Georgian classification, GEL settlement, responsible-use design, or NBG engagement. The model still needs local governance.

**Financial attractiveness: 3 / 5**
Speed to market may improve early testing, but revenue share, licensing costs, external platform control, and weaker wallet ownership reduce the upside captured by TBC.

**Execution feasibility: 4 / 5**
This option may be technically faster because an external provider brings product knowledge. However, legal responsibility, data ownership, user protection, and regulator-facing governance remain difficult.

### Option 3: Crypto/VASP-first standalone platform

**Strategic control: 2 / 5**
A crypto-first model may move wallet behavior outside TBC. If users fund through crypto rails or external wallets, TBC loses the central strategic advantage.

**Regulatory and trust fit: 2 / 5**
VASP registration may help with digital-asset activity, but it does not automatically resolve event-contract classification. The product may also look more speculative and less bank-grade.

**Financial attractiveness: 3 / 5**
Crypto-native users may trade actively, and turnover velocity could be high. However, mainstream adoption, GEL payment flow, responsible-use credibility, and bank trust are weaker.

**Execution feasibility: 3 / 5**
A crypto-first pilot may be faster technically, but it introduces AML, custody, conversion, customer education, and reputational challenges.

# 6. Recommended Option

The recommended option is:

> Option 1: TBC-owned GEL wallet platform under an NBG special-designation or sandbox-first route.

This option should be implemented through an 18-month controlled launch path, not immediate public launch.

---

## 6.1 Supporting reason 1: It best solves the wallet ownership problem

The professor’s feedback correctly reframes the case. The platform is not only about trading-fee revenue. It is about whether TBC owns the next risk-active wallet layer.

If users fund foreign platforms, crypto wallets, or betting accounts, TBC loses balances, payment flow, customer engagement, and behavioral insight. If users access event contracts through a TBC-linked wallet, TBC keeps the funding and settlement relationship inside its ecosystem.

This finding connects directly to the platform strategy:

> The greatest structural threat is a platform that captures the wallet before TBC does.

Option 1 is the only option that fully addresses this threat.

---

## 6.2 Supporting reason 2: It has the strongest regulatory and trust fit

Prediction markets immediately raise a classification problem. Without a clear regulatory pathway, the product may be viewed as gambling-like speculation.

Option 1 gives TBC the strongest path to separate the product from betting-style positioning. It allows the platform to be designed around:

* NBG engagement;
* limited event categories;
* official data sources;
* GEL settlement;
* bank-grade KYC and AML;
* responsible-use controls;
* market surveillance;
* transparent outcome resolution;
* audit logs and regulator-ready reporting.

This connects directly to the governance workstream and responsible AI plan. The product should not scale before the control layer is proven.

---

## 6.3 Supporting reason 3: It captures the strongest financial upside

The updated ROI model shows that the opportunity is financially meaningful if the platform captures a small but meaningful share of existing risk-active behavior.

The base case assumes:

| Base-case metric                 |         Value |
| -------------------------------- | ------------: |
| Risk-active user proxy           | 651,000 users |
| Adoption                         |    10 percent |
| Monthly active users             |        65,100 |
| Monthly turnover per active user |     GEL 6,546 |
| Monthly platform turnover        |    GEL 426.1M |
| Take rate                        |     1 percent |
| Monthly gross revenue            |     GEL 4.26M |
| Annual benefit at stabilization  |    GEL 27.95M |

| Payback period                   |    8.9 months |

| IRR                              |  99.9 percent |

This upside is strongest when TBC owns distribution and payment flow. Bank-backed mobile access can reduce CAC, GEL wallet settlement can reduce payment friction, and trusted brand positioning can improve adoption.

# Part 2. Recommended Business Model

## 7. Business model summary

The recommended business model is:

> A TBC-owned wallet marketplace for regulated event contracts.

Users are not buying stocks or crypto assets. They are taking positions on clearly defined public outcomes. The platform gives them a simple way to trade Yes or No on verifiable events, with defined downside, transparent market pricing, and official-source resolution.

For TBC, the product is not only a new trading feature. It is a wallet, engagement, and platform-control strategy.

---

## 8. Value proposition in full

## 8.1 Value proposition for users

The user value proposition is:

> Simple event exposure inside a trusted bank-linked environment.

Users receive:

* simple Yes/No contracts on verifiable Georgian public events;
* controlled-stakes access with defined downside;
* transparent pricing through market-implied probability;
* clear settlement rules;
* official-source outcome resolution;
* lower complexity than traditional brokerage;
* stronger trust and local relevance than offshore crypto or foreign platforms;
* responsible-use controls, risk warnings, and support routes.

The first market categories should be official-source and bank-relevant:

* inflation releases;
* GEL exchange-rate bands;
* NBG policy decisions;
* election turnout;
* official macroeconomic releases;
* other clearly verifiable public-data outcomes.

The product should avoid:

* sports-betting-style positioning;
* “easy money” language;
* leaderboards during sandbox;
* vague public-event claims;
* markets without official sources;
* vulnerable-user targeting.

## 8.2 Value proposition for TBC

The TBC value proposition is:

> TBC owns the wallet, funding flow, customer relationship, and governance model for a new risk-active retail category.

TBC gains:

* wallet ownership;
* retained balances inside the TBC ecosystem;
* payment and settlement flow;
* lower CAC through existing mobile banking distribution;
* new retail engagement beyond deposits, cards, and brokerage;
* first-mover position in regulated Georgian event contracts;
* market-intelligence layer on event demand and aggregate expectations;
* ability to protect users through bank-grade controls;
* opportunity to prevent foreign platforms, crypto wallets, and betting operators from defining the category first.

The strongest business logic is not only trading fees. The stronger logic is that TBC can prevent leakage of risk-active behavior away from the bank ecosystem.

## 8.3 Value proposition for regulators

The regulator value proposition is:

> A controlled, auditable, bank-backed market design instead of an unregulated or betting-style alternative.

Regulators receive:

* sandbox-first or special-designation pathway;
* bank-grade KYC and AML;
* exposure caps and responsible-use rules;
* official-source event resolution;
* transparent market approval process;
* restricted event taxonomy;
* market surveillance;
* user appeal process;
* regulator-ready reporting;
* audit trail for high-impact decisions.

The product should be positioned as a supervised event-contract marketplace, not as a gambling substitute.

## 8.4 Value proposition for market makers and liquidity providers

Liquidity providers receive:

* defined market categories;
* transparent market rules;
* official-source resolution;
* trading-volume opportunity;
* market-maker incentive or subsidy during sandbox;
* market-quality scorecards;
* clear quote obligations and monitoring rules.

Liquidity providers matter because prediction markets fail if users see empty markets or wide spreads.

## 8.5 Value proposition for payment partners

Payment partners receive:

* bank-backed wallet control;
* clear KYC and AML structure;
* defined funding and withdrawal rules;
* payment-hold escalation process;
* fraud monitoring;
* reporting evidence;
* lower reputational risk than unregulated betting-style or offshore alternatives.

# 9. Operating Model Changes by Function

Product and mobile banking teams normally focus on feature delivery, user experience, adoption, and app engagement.

### Required change

For the prediction-market platform, the mobile app becomes a controlled access point, not only a distribution channel.

Product and mobile banking teams must design:

* controlled product placement inside or adjacent to the mobile banking app;
* sandbox eligibility gate;
* product education screen;
* risk quiz or user acknowledgement;
* wallet funding and withdrawal flow;
* official-source labels on markets;
* maximum-loss display before order placement;
* position dashboard;
* settlement-status display;
* responsible-use messages;
* dispute and support entry point;
* app-release governance checklist.

### Strategic implication

The product cannot be treated like a normal app feature. The mobile app must make the platform understandable, but not frictionless in a way that encourages impulsive trading.

# 10. Revenue Model Logic

## 10.1 Core revenue formula

The core revenue formula is:

```text
Active traders × monthly turnover per active user × take rate = monthly gross revenue
```

The base case is:

```text
65,100 active users × GEL 6,546 monthly turnover × 1% take rate = GEL 4.26M monthly gross revenue
```

The model is driven by turnover velocity, not only by deposits. Users can recycle balances through multiple short-duration contracts.

## 10.2 Primary revenue stream

The primary revenue stream should be:

* spread capture;
* maker/taker fee;
* hybrid trading fee structure.

The fee structure should be simple and transparent. It should not be hidden in a way that damages trust.

## 10.3 Future revenue options

Future optional revenue streams may include:

* compliant market-creation fees;
* premium market tools;
* B2B market-intelligence products;
* institutional event-data products;
* liquidity services;
* analytics products.

These should remain future options until retail liquidity and regulatory comfort are proven.

## 10.4 Main cost drivers

The main cost drivers are:

* market-maker or liquidity subsidy;
* payment processing cost;
* compliance and legal run cost;
* market surveillance;
* wallet and KYC operations;
* platform operations;
* customer support;
* data and AI infrastructure;
* customer acquisition;
* change management.

## 10.5 Strategic interpretation

The business model is viable only if TBC reduces cost and increases trust through its existing assets:

* mobile app distribution reduces CAC;
* TBC wallet reduces payment friction;
* KYC infrastructure reduces onboarding cost;
* bank trust supports adoption;
* GEL settlement supports mainstream use;

* trading activity;
* event taxonomy;
* official source registry;

* market surveillance alerts;
* support tickets;
* dispute records;
* outcome-resolution evidence;
* human override logs;
* regulator and payment-partner reporting logs;
* mobile app journey analytics.

## 11.2 Official source registry

Every market must have an official source before listing.

Initial sources may include:

* NBG for policy-rate decisions and official exchange-rate data;
* Geostat for inflation and economic releases;
* CEC for election turnout and official election results;
* approved FX reference sources for exchange-rate bands;
* official government or regulator publications for narrowly defined civic outcomes.

The rule should be:

```text
No official source, no market.
```

## 11.3 AI use cases required before scale

The highest-priority AI use cases are:

1. market surveillance and manipulation detection;
2. responsible-use scoring and intervention routing;
3. payment, KYC, wallet, and fraud-risk monitoring;
4. AI-assisted market explanations and risk disclosures;
5. event eligibility screening;
6. outcome-resolution support;
7. dispute triage and support routing;
8. liquidity and market-maker monitoring;
9. regulatory and payment-partner reporting automation.

## 11.4 AI principles

AI should follow four principles:

1. **AI supports decisions, but humans own them.**
   AI may screen, flag, draft, score, summarize, and route. It should not independently approve markets, freeze accounts, approve payouts, or submit external reports.

2. **AI protects trust before optimizing growth.**
   The platform should not use AI to push users toward more trading or deposits.

3. **Every high-impact AI output must be auditable.**
   The system must log input data, model output, confidence score, human reviewer, final decision, reason code, and user notice where applicable.

4. **Mobile app messages must be clear.**
   If AI affects a user’s access, deposit, withdrawal, trading, payout, or dispute handling, the app must show a clear explanation and route to human review.

## 11.5 Infrastructure requirements

The infrastructure should include:

* KYC and AML integration;
* user limit engine;
* responsible-use rules engine;

* AI model register;

## 11.6 Strategic implication

Data and AI are not optional add-ons. They are part of the operating model required to make the platform acceptable to TBC, users, regulators, payment partners, and the board.

# 12. Final Recommendation

The recommended strategy is to proceed with Option 1.

TBC should build a controlled, TBC-owned, GEL-settled prediction-market platform under an NBG special-designation or sandbox-first path. The product should be integrated into or adjacent to the mobile banking ecosystem, with TBC retaining control over wallet funding, KYC, GEL settlement, responsible-use rules, official-source resolution, market surveillance, data governance, and reporting.

The recommendation is not immediate public launch.

The recommendation is to fund the 18-month controlled path that proves whether the model works before scaling.

Public launch should proceed only if sandbox evidence supports:

* regulatory comfort;
* user adoption;
* turnover velocity;
* wallet funding behavior;
* low payment friction;
* liquidity quality;
* responsible-use performance;
* dispute control;
* customer support readiness;
* contribution margin close to the base case.

If sandbox evidence is closer to the severe downside scenario, the board should continue the sandbox, pivot the model, or stop the project.
