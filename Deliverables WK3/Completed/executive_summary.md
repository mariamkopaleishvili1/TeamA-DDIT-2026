# Executive Summary

## Project

Team A: Georgian Prediction-Market Platform  
Industry: Fintech  
Market: Republic of Georgia  
Primary client assumption: TBC-backed launch model  
Recommended strategic option: TBC-funded, GEL-settled prediction-market platform under an NBG special-designation or sandbox-first route

---

## Executive Summary

This project is not only a fintech product proposal. It is a strategic decision about whether TBC should own the next layer of risk-active retail engagement in Georgia or allow that behavior to move to betting platforms, crypto exchanges, foreign prediction-market platforms, and non-bank wallets.

Georgia already has the behavior that prediction markets need. Users are active in betting, crypto, and retail trading. These channels show demand for short-term views, fast feedback, and high-turnover financial behavior. The problem is that the existing channels do not strengthen the core banking relationship. Betting platforms capture attention and cash flow but carry reputational risk. Crypto platforms capture speculative behavior but weaken bank control over balances and identity. Brokerage is legitimate but too complex for many entry-level users. International prediction-market platforms can bring product know-how, but they would likely own the customer relationship, product data, and platform economics.

The recommendation is to approve an **18-month controlled launch path** for a **TBC-backed, GEL-settled prediction-market platform**, assuming NBG is open to exploring a special designation or sandbox route for event-contract markets. This is not a recommendation for immediate public launch. It is a recommendation for TBC to fund and control the platform architecture before another player defines the category.

The strongest version of the opportunity is not a copy of Polymarket or Kalshi. It is a Georgian model built around TBC’s core advantages: trusted brand, customer base, mobile app distribution, KYC infrastructure, payment rails, GEL balances, compliance capability, and regulator access. The platform should create a controlled event-contract marketplace where users can take positions on narrow, official-source public outcomes while keeping funds inside a bank-linked wallet environment.

The strategic objective is therefore simple:

> TBC should not only sponsor the platform. TBC should own the wallet, fund the controlled marketplace, and capture the deposit, engagement, data, and fee economics created by the new category.

---

## The Client Case for TBC

For TBC, the central question is not whether prediction markets are interesting. The central question is whether TBC wants to own or lose the customer relationship around risk-active retail behavior.

A large part of consumer attention is already moving toward high-frequency, event-based financial activity. Users who might otherwise hold deposits, save, invest, or remain inside the banking ecosystem are already allocating time and money to betting, crypto, and trading substitutes. A prediction-market platform gives TBC a way to bring part of that behavior into a regulated, transparent, GEL-settled environment.

This matters for the core banking business in five ways.

First, the platform can create a new wallet layer. If users fund event contracts through a TBC-linked wallet, TBC can keep balances, payment flows, identity, and settlement inside its ecosystem. This is strategically different from users funding offshore exchanges or betting accounts.

Second, the platform can improve retail engagement. Traditional deposits and brokerage products are not enough to keep younger and risk-active users engaged daily or weekly. Event contracts create repeat engagement around public outcomes, macro events, exchange rates, inflation releases, elections, and other official data.

Third, the platform can lower acquisition cost. A standalone fintech would need to build trust, acquire users, integrate payments, and create KYC processes from scratch. TBC already has app distribution, customer trust, payment access, onboarding infrastructure, and brand credibility.

Fourth, the platform can create new market intelligence. Trading behavior around inflation, FX expectations, elections, and public outcomes can create a new layer of aggregate market sentiment. This should be handled carefully and in line with data-protection rules, but it can become a strategic information asset.

Fifth, the platform protects against future platform displacement. If Polymarket, Kalshi, a crypto exchange, or a local betting operator defines this category first, TBC risks becoming only a payment rail. The economic value would move to the platform owner, while TBC would carry payment and reputational exposure without owning the product economics.

## Why TBC Should Not Only Partner With International Platforms

International players are useful benchmarks, but they are not the preferred ownership model.

Polymarket demonstrates demand for event-based trading and crypto-native liquidity. Kalshi demonstrates the value of a regulated exchange model. However, neither model transfers cleanly into the Georgian market. Polymarket’s crypto-native structure does not solve Georgian regulatory classification, GEL settlement, local consumer protection, or bank-grade responsible-use requirements. Kalshi’s U.S. regulated-exchange model is built around a different legal and market infrastructure.

A simple partnership with an international player would create four strategic weaknesses for TBC.

First, TBC would not own the customer relationship. The international platform would likely control the product experience, user behavior data, market design, and pricing power.

Second, TBC would not fully own the wallet layer. Funds could move into external wallets, stablecoins, or platform-controlled balances, weakening TBC’s ability to retain deposits and payment flows.

Third, the regulatory model would still need to be Georgian. Even if an international platform provides technology, NBG and Georgian payment partners would still require local controls, source rules, dispute handling, KYC, reporting, and responsible-use design.

Fourth, TBC would lose the first-mover advantage in defining the category locally. The strategic value is not only in offering prediction markets. The strategic value is in becoming the trusted Georgian institution that defines how prediction markets should operate.

International partnership should therefore be treated as a possible technology, liquidity, or knowledge-support option, not as the core strategy. TBC should control the local platform, wallet, customer relationship, governance model, and regulatory pathway.

## Three Conclusions

### 1. Georgia has a real market gap for regulated event contracts

Georgia already has risk-active behavior across gambling, cryptocurrency, and retail trading. These activities show that users are familiar with speculation, digital financial activity, and high-turnover behavior. However, the existing channels do not create a trusted local event-contract category.

Gambling has scale but creates reputational and regulatory concerns for a bank-backed product. Crypto has speculation and velocity but lacks mainstream regulatory comfort. Brokerage has legitimacy but is too complex for many users who want simple event exposure. Banks have trust, payments, KYC, and distribution, but do not currently offer a simple, regulated market for trading public outcomes.

This creates the white space: a platform that is **simpler than brokerage, more controlled than betting, more mainstream than crypto, and more valuable to TBC than letting users leave the bank ecosystem**.

### 2. Regulation is the unlock, but ownership is the strategic prize

The project assumes that NBG may be open to exploring a special designation or sandbox route for a controlled prediction-market platform. This assumption changes the strategic question.

The question is no longer whether Georgia should copy Polymarket or Kalshi. Their models are not interchangeable. A Georgian model would need to be shaped by local regulation, payment rails, banking trust, consumer protection expectations, responsible-use controls, and GEL settlement.

The recent discussion around GELT, a proposed Georgian lari token, also shows that Georgia’s digital-money and digital-payment environment is evolving. This does not mean the prediction-market platform should become crypto-first. It means TBC should prepare for a future where wallets, tokenized GEL settlement, and bank-linked digital balances become more strategically important.

The strongest route is therefore not a crypto-first launch, a white-label foreign platform, or a standalone fintech app. The strongest route is a **TBC-owned, bank-backed, regulator-led, GEL-settled model** where the platform is designed from the beginning as a supervised marketplace.

### 3. The ROI case is strong, but only if TBC captures the wallet and trust economics

The updated ROI model shows that the opportunity can be financially attractive. The model is based on a risk-active market proxy of **651,000 users** and average monthly turnover velocity of **GEL 6,546 per active user**. Turnover velocity is important because prediction-market users can recycle balances across short-duration contracts. The model does not require users to deposit GEL 6,546 per month. It requires active users to generate trading volume through repeated market participation.

The base case assumes 10 percent adoption of the risk-active pool, equal to **65,100 monthly active users**. Under this case, the platform generates:

| Base-case metric | Value |
|---|---:|
| Monthly active users | 65,100 |
| Monthly platform turnover | GEL 426.1M |
| Monthly gross revenue | GEL 4.26M |
| Annual benefit at stabilization | GEL 27.95M |
| Total investment to recover | GEL 20.81M |
| Payback period | 8.9 months |
| IRR | 99.9 percent |
| 5-year NPV at 18 percent discount rate | GEL 57.99M |
| Year 1 capital requirement | GEL 12.00M |
| Year 1 CapEx | GEL 7.40M |
| Year 1 OpEx | GEL 4.60M |

The financial case is attractive, but it is not automatic. The model depends on user adoption, turnover velocity, low payment cost, sufficient liquidity, trusted event resolution, responsible-use controls, and regulator comfort. The reason TBC matters is that these conditions are much harder to achieve without a bank sponsor that controls distribution, identity, payment rails, and trust.

## Cost of Inaction

The cost of inaction is not only lost revenue. It is loss of category control.

If TBC does not fund the platform, three outcomes become more likely.

First, international prediction-market platforms may define the product category before Georgian banks do. In that case, TBC may become a payment provider or compliance gatekeeper rather than the owner of the customer relationship.

Second, betting and crypto substitutes may continue capturing risk-active retail behavior outside the banking ecosystem. This weakens TBC’s ability to retain deposits, observe customer needs, and offer regulated alternatives.

Third, another Georgian financial institution may use the NBG special-designation route first. If that happens, the first mover can build the trusted event-contract brand, own liquidity relationships, and set the operating standards.

The base-case annual benefit at stabilization is **GEL 27.95M**. A six-month delay therefore represents roughly **GEL 14.0M of foregone annualized benefit**, before considering strategic loss of customer attention, wallet balances, and category ownership.

The board should treat delay as a strategic risk. The platform does not need immediate public launch, but the controlled path should begin before the market is defined by external players.

---

## Recommended Strategy

The recommended strategy is to pursue **Option 1: a TBC-backed NBG special-designation or sandbox-first route**.

This option is preferred because it solves the platform’s most important constraints together:

- **Regulatory legitimacy:** NBG engagement gives the product a credible path to classification and supervision.
- **Wallet ownership:** TBC can keep customer funding, balances, and settlement inside a bank-linked GEL wallet environment.
- **Trust and distribution:** TBC backing provides brand trust, app access, KYC infrastructure, and customer reach.
- **Payment economics:** GEL settlement and bank-linked rails can reduce payment friction and protect margins.
- **Responsible-use credibility:** Bank-grade controls help separate the platform from betting-style positioning.
- **Category ownership:** TBC can define the Georgian event-contract market before foreign or non-bank platforms do.

The VASP or USDC route should remain a fallback, not the base case. It may support a narrower crypto-native model, but it does not solve event-contract legality or mainstream trust. A standalone fintech route is also weaker because it would face higher CAC, weaker trust, harder payment access, and a slower regulatory path.

## Business Model Logic

The platform is a marketplace for event contracts.

Users trade positions on clearly defined outcomes, such as whether inflation exceeds a threshold, whether the GEL exchange rate enters a defined range, or whether an official public event reaches a measurable result. The user-facing product is simple: yes or no, fixed downside, transparent probability, and clear settlement.

The business value for TBC comes from the system around the trade:

- a TBC-linked wallet or account funding layer;
- GEL settlement where possible;
- full KYC and AML coverage;
- controlled event categories;
- official-source resolution;
- market surveillance;
- responsible-use controls;
- liquidity and market-maker management;
- customer support and dispute handling;
- regulator and payment-partner reporting.

The revenue model is based on trading turnover. The base case uses a 1 percent blended take rate from fees, spread capture, or a hybrid structure. Payment cost is applied to funding flow, not full trading turnover, because users can recycle balances inside the platform.

The platform can also support future optional revenue streams, such as compliant market-creation fees, analytics products, premium features, or B2B/institutional products. These should not be treated as core base-case revenue until retail liquidity is proven.

---

## Wallet and Deposit Logic

The wallet layer is central to the business case.

A prediction-market product creates repeated funding, trading, settlement, withdrawal, and balance-recycling behavior. If TBC owns the wallet layer, the platform can strengthen the core bank relationship rather than weaken it.

The desired model is:

1. users fund a TBC-linked GEL wallet or account;
2. users trade event contracts inside the controlled platform;
3. balances remain inside the TBC ecosystem unless withdrawn;
4. TBC controls KYC, AML, payment monitoring, and responsible-use triggers;
5. TBC captures engagement data, payment flow, and potential deposit stickiness;
6. public launch is approved only if the wallet, risk, and liquidity model performs in sandbox.

GELT and similar digital-lari infrastructure should be monitored as part of the future settlement environment. If a compliant GEL-backed token becomes available, it may improve settlement design or wallet programmability. However, the base-case model should remain bank-controlled and GEL-settled rather than dependent on a crypto-native route.

## AI and Governance Logic

AI should be used first as a control layer, not as a prediction engine.

The most important AI use cases are:

- event eligibility screening;
- contract explanation and risk-disclosure support;
- market surveillance and manipulation detection;
- responsible-use scoring;
- payment, KYC, and fraud-risk monitoring;
- outcome-resolution support;
- dispute triage;
- liquidity and market-maker monitoring;
- regulatory and payment-partner reporting automation.

These use cases are essential because prediction markets depend on trust. Users must trust that contracts are clear, markets are monitored, outcomes are resolved correctly, and restrictions are applied fairly.

Governance should be led by a **Prediction Market Governance and AI Risk Committee**. The committee should include Product, Compliance, Legal, Market Operations, Data and AI, Payments and KYC, Responsible Use, Customer Trust, Information Security, and a bank sponsor representative.

High-impact AI outputs should not create final adverse decisions without human review. Human override is required for cases such as market pauses, account restrictions, payout disputes, extended payment holds, responsible-use interventions, and regulator-facing reports.

## Roadmap Logic

The roadmap is not a normal product rollout. It is a sequence of proof points.

### H1: Months 1 to 6

The first phase makes the platform legally and operationally testable. It should deliver:

- governance committee charter;
- NBG pre-consultation and legal classification package;
- official event source registry;
- approved market taxonomy;
- contract templates and risk disclosures;
- payment, KYC, AML, and GEL wallet design;
- responsible-use framework;
- MVP trading and admin workflow.

### H2: Months 7 to 18

The second phase tests the platform under controlled conditions. It should deliver:

- controlled sandbox pilot;
- liquidity and market-maker programme;
- market surveillance and responsible-use monitoring;
- outcome-resolution and reporting workflow;
- dispute handling;
- unit-economics validation;
- board launch gate.

### H3: Post-18-month option space

Public launch should be treated as an option, not an automatic continuation. At month 18, the board should decide whether to launch, continue sandbox, pivot to a fallback route, or stop.

## Key Risks and Controls

The platform has three major risks.

### 1. Regulatory or gambling reclassification risk

The platform may be viewed as gambling if event categories, user protections, or market structure are weak.

**Control:** NBG engagement, special-designation pathway, narrow event taxonomy, official-source contracts, responsible-use controls, and legal review before public launch.

### 2. Liquidity and market-quality risk

Users will not trust the platform if markets are empty, spreads are wide, or exits are difficult.

**Control:** market-maker programme, spread thresholds, liquidity monitoring, market-quality dashboard, and market-level launch gates.

### 3. User harm and reputational risk

A bank-backed platform cannot appear to target vulnerable users or gambling-excluded segments.

**Control:** KYC, responsible-use screening, deposit alerts, loss limits, cooling-off periods, adverse-action notices, human appeal process, and responsible-use monitoring.

## Board Decision Required

The board should approve the controlled launch path, not unconditional public launch.

The required decisions are:

1. **Approve the NBG special-designation or sandbox engagement path.**
2. **Approve the TBC-backed, GEL-settled platform model as the recommended route.**
3. **Approve TBC ownership of the wallet and funding layer, rather than outsourcing the customer relationship to an international platform.**
4. **Approve the Year 1 capital requirement of GEL 12.00M, including GEL 7.40M CapEx and GEL 4.60M OpEx.**
5. **Approve the governance and change-management setup required before sandbox launch.**
6. **Require a month-18 board gate before any public launch decision.**

The launch gate should be based on evidence, not assumptions. Public launch should proceed only if sandbox data confirms adoption, turnover velocity, payment cost, liquidity quality, responsible-use performance, dispute resolution, and contribution margin.

---

## Final Position

A Georgian prediction-market platform can be a financially attractive and strategically defensible fintech opportunity, but only under a controlled design.

For TBC, the strongest argument is not only new fee revenue. The stronger argument is ownership of the next risk-active wallet layer. If TBC funds and governs the platform, it can keep balances, identity, trust, payment flows, and market intelligence inside its ecosystem. If it waits, the category may be defined by foreign platforms, betting operators, crypto exchanges, or another Georgian financial institution.

The platform should not be framed as a betting substitute or a crypto-native experiment. It should be framed as a regulated event-contract marketplace, backed by bank trust, GEL rails, wallet ownership, official-source resolution, responsible-use controls, market surveillance, and clear governance.

The investment case is strong in the base scenario, but the correct decision is staged. The board should fund the controlled path that proves whether the model works before committing to public scale.
