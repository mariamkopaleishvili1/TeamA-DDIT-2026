# Platform Strategy Canvas v1

## Purpose

This canvas maps the client's position in the platform competitive structure and produces a specific strategic recommendation: build, join, defend, or acquire.

## Instructions

Complete during the Saturday Platform Strategy Canvas workshop. Use the Platform Revolution concepts from the afternoon teaching block and your industry knowledge from Weekend 1.

# Option 1: TBC-Backed Regulatory Sandbox Pathway

## Section 1. Value unit

What is the minimum unit of interaction the platform could facilitate in this industry? What changes hands between the two sides?

- Value unit: GEL-settled event contract on macroeconomic or civic outcomes
- Description: The minimum unit is a simple YES/NO event contract linked to a measurable outcome such as GEL/USD movement or local election results. Users take a position on whether the event will happen and the contract value reflects the market's expected probability. The product is designed to feel like a regulated financial tool, not a betting product.

---

## Section 2. Producer side

Who produces value on the platform? What do they get from participation?

- Producer type: Information-rich traders, market makers and liquidity providers
- What they contribute:
  - Capital to support trading activity
  - Liquidity for event contracts
  - Local knowledge about macroeconomic and civic outcomes
  - Better price discovery through informed trading
- What they receive:
  - Opportunity to earn from superior analysis
  - Access to simple local event markets
  - A regulated-feeling trading environment
  - Trust from TBC-backed infrastructure and NBG-reviewed rules
- Current number or market size: The producer side would initially be narrow and controlled. It could include professional traders, market makers, financial analysts and users with strong local information. Scale should be tested through a pilot before public launch.

---

## Section 3. Consumer side

Who consumes value on the platform? What do they get from participation?

- Consumer type: Mainstream Georgian retail users and information seekers
- What they seek:
  - Simple access to local event-based financial products
  - Clear probabilities on events they understand
  - GEL settlement instead of crypto-only settlement
  - A trusted platform connected to a known banking brand
- What they receive:
  - Easy-to-understand YES/NO contracts
  - Transparent probability pricing
  - Bank-backed trust and payment rails
  - A safer experience with KYC/AML and responsible-use controls
- Current number or market size: The main opportunity is mainstream Georgian adoption. TBC's existing digital customer base and payment infrastructure can help the platform reach users who would not normally trust a standalone prediction-market startup.

---

## Section 4. Network effect mechanism

- Primary network effect type: Indirect network effects with data network effects
- Mechanism description: More consumers create more trading activity, which attracts more liquidity providers. More liquidity improves prices and reduces spreads, which makes the platform more useful for users. Over time, the platform can become a local signal for macroeconomic and civic expectations.
- Critical mass threshold: The first target should be a controlled pilot with enough users and liquidity to prove that markets can stay active, prices can update clearly and users understand the product.
- Risk of negative network effects:
  - If liquidity is weak, users may lose trust in the platform
  - If markets are one-sided, price discovery may become poor
  - If the product looks like gambling, regulatory and reputational risk will increase

---

## Section 5. Client position

Where does the client sit today?

- Current role: potential platform operator
- Current leverage in the market:
  - TBC-backed trust
  - Payment rails
  - Compliance capacity
  - Customer access
  - Potential NBG sandbox pathway
- Data assets available:
  - Public macroeconomic and civic data
  - Possible TBC Capital research inputs
  - Future transaction and behavior data from the pilot
- Customer relationship strength: Strong if launched through TBC because customers already trust the banking brand and payment infrastructure.

---

## Section 6. Strategic recommendation

Choose one: Build / Join / Defend / Acquire

- Recommendation: Build through a regulatory-first sandbox pathway
- Reason 1 (client capabilities): TBC has the trust, payment rails, compliance capacity and customer access needed to make the product credible.
- Reason 2 (competitive dynamics): A TBC-backed structure creates a stronger position than standalone fintech or crypto-native entrants because it combines regulation, distribution and trust.
- Reason 3 (financial logic): TBC's existing customer base and infrastructure can reduce customer acquisition cost and improve the chance of mainstream adoption.

---

## Section 7. First move

What is the one specific action the client must take in the next 6 months?

- Action: Submit a sandbox or pre-consultation proposal to NBG asking whether limited macroeconomic and civic event contracts can be tested as a supervised financial innovation.
- Owner: CEO or Regulatory Lead with external legal counsel and TBC compliance support
- Success metric: NBG provides written feedback on whether GEL-settled event contracts can be tested and what restrictions, caps and consumer-protection controls are required.
- Investment required (order of magnitude): Medium investment for legal work, compliance design, pilot product development, market-maker planning and user testing.

# Option 2: VASP-Registered Crypto-Native Platform

## Section 1. Value unit

What is the minimum unit of interaction the platform could facilitate in this industry? What changes hands between the two sides?

- Value unit: Crypto-settled event contract or prediction token
- Description: The minimum unit is a tokenized YES/NO event contract settled through USDC or another stablecoin. Users take a position on a specific event and the contract can be traded before the result is known. This option positions the product as a crypto-native prediction-market platform.

---

## Section 2. Producer side

Who produces value on the platform? What do they get from participation?

- Producer type: VASP partners, crypto-native traders, liquidity providers and market makers
- What they contribute:
  - Stablecoin liquidity
  - Crypto trading infrastructure
  - Wallet and settlement support
  - Market-making activity
- What they receive:
  - Trading fee opportunities
  - Access to a new crypto-native product category
  - Early position in a niche market
  - Potential yield or spread-based income from liquidity provision
- Current number or market size: The producer side is smaller than the bank-backed option because it depends on crypto-native users, VASP partners and stablecoin liquidity. It may be easier to launch technically, but the audience is narrower.

---

## Section 3. Consumer side

Who consumes value on the platform? What do they get from participation?

- Consumer type: Crypto-native Georgian users and digitally active traders
- What they seek:
  - Faster access to event contracts
  - Stablecoin settlement
  - A product similar to global crypto prediction markets
  - Flexible trading outside traditional banking rails
- What they receive:
  - USDC or stablecoin-based event contracts
  - Faster settlement
  - Crypto-native wallet access
  - Exposure to prediction tokens or convertible virtual assets
- Current number or market size: The consumer side is narrower than mainstream Georgian banking users. This option mainly targets people who already understand crypto, stablecoins and digital wallets.

---

## Section 4. Network effect mechanism

- Primary network effect type: Indirect network effects with liquidity network effects
- Mechanism description: More liquidity providers create better spreads and more active markets, which attracts more crypto-native users. More users create more trading volume, which makes the platform more attractive for market makers and VASP partners.
- Critical mass threshold: The platform would need several reliable VASP partners, stable settlement rails and enough active traders to keep markets liquid.
- Risk of negative network effects:
  - Too many markets could fragment liquidity
  - Stablecoin risk could reduce user confidence
  - NBG may later restrict event-based virtual assets or fiat off-ramps
  - Crypto-native competitors could copy the model quickly

---

## Section 5. Client position

Where does the client sit today?

- Current role: potential platform operator
- Current leverage in the market:
  - Possible VASP registration route
  - Faster technical launch path
  - Ability to use stablecoin settlement
  - Potential partnerships with crypto service providers
- Data assets available:
  - Public event outcome data
  - Blockchain transaction data
  - Future trading and liquidity data from the platform
- Customer relationship strength: Medium to low. The platform would need to build trust with crypto users and also maintain relationships with VASP partners and regulators.

---

## Section 6. Strategic recommendation

Choose one: Build / Join / Defend / Acquire

- Recommendation: Build only as a fallback or niche route
- Reason 1 (client capabilities): The VASP route may be easier to launch technically, but it does not automatically solve the legal classification of event contracts.
- Reason 2 (competitive dynamics): Crypto-native competitors can copy the model and the audience is narrower than the mainstream banking audience.
- Reason 3 (financial logic): Stablecoin settlement may reduce some payment friction, but ROI depends on enough crypto-native users, strong liquidity and regulatory comfort.

---

## Section 7. First move

What is the one specific action the client must take in the next 6 months?

- Action: Submit a VASP pre-consultation request to NBG asking whether crypto-settled event contracts can be offered as virtual-asset instruments and what restrictions would apply.
- Owner: CEO or Regulatory Lead with external legal counsel and VASP partners
- Success metric: NBG provides written feedback on whether event-based virtual assets are allowed and whether fiat off-ramps for prediction-market winnings can be supported.
- Investment required (order of magnitude): Medium investment for legal consultation, VASP registration planning, technical MVP development, stablecoin settlement design and liquidity partnerships.

---

- Are the three justification reasons distinct and evidence-based? Yes, they cover client capabilities, competitive dynamics and financial logic.

# Option 3: Standalone Fintech Launch

## Section 1. Value unit

What is the minimum unit of interaction the platform could facilitate in this industry? What changes hands between the two sides?

- Value unit: Independent fintech event contract
- Description: The minimum unit is a simple event contract offered by an independent Georgian fintech brand. Users take a YES/NO position on a local macroeconomic or civic event and the platform manages trading, settlement, rules and customer experience without initial bank backing.

---

## Section 2. Producer side

Who produces value on the platform? What do they get from participation?

- Producer type: Independent market makers, early adopters, analysts and liquidity providers
- What they contribute:
  - Initial liquidity
  - Trading activity
  - Market insight
  - Early feedback on product design and rules
- What they receive:
  - Early access to a new local event-contract platform
  - Potential trading profit
  - Influence over early market design
  - Possible long-term upside if the platform grows
- Current number or market size: The producer side would start small because the platform has no existing distribution, no bank-backed trust and no proven liquidity base.

---

## Section 3. Consumer side

Who consumes value on the platform? What do they get from participation?

- Consumer type: Early fintech adopters and Georgian retail users interested in simple local event markets
- What they seek:
  - Easy access to local event contracts
  - A simple alternative to stocks, bonds or crypto derivatives
  - Transparent rules and clear outcomes
  - A low-friction digital experience
- What they receive:
  - Independent event-contract platform
  - Simple local markets
  - Full product experience controlled by the fintech
  - Education and responsible-use controls
- Current number or market size: The potential market could be meaningful over time, but early adoption would likely be slow because the platform must build trust, payments, liquidity and regulatory credibility from zero.

---

## Section 4. Network effect mechanism

- Primary network effect type: Indirect network effects with trust-based local network effects
- Mechanism description: More users create more trading activity, which attracts more liquidity providers. More liquidity improves the experience and can bring more users. However, because the platform is standalone, trust-building is slower and the network effect may take longer to start.
- Critical mass threshold: The platform would need a narrow controlled pilot with one or two simple local event markets, enough liquidity to avoid inactive markets and strong early user confidence.
- Risk of negative network effects:
  - Slow early growth could make markets look empty
  - Weak liquidity could reduce trust
  - Payment partners may hesitate without bank backing
  - Regulators or users may question whether the product is safe and credible

---

## Section 5. Client position

Where does the client sit today?

- Current role: potential platform operator
- Current leverage in the market:
  - Full independence
  - Full product control
  - Ability to shape brand, pricing and growth strategy from zero
  - Flexibility to test a small pilot
- Data assets available:
  - None currently
  - Public macroeconomic and civic data
  - Future user behavior and trading data from the pilot
- Customer relationship strength: Low at launch. The company would need to build its own customer base, payment partnerships, liquidity strategy and regulatory relationship from zero.

---

## Section 6. Strategic recommendation

Choose one: Build / Join / Defend / Acquire

- Recommendation: Do not build as the starting route
- Reason 1 (client capabilities): The company would have to build customer trust, compliance, payment rails, liquidity and regulatory relationships without a strong partner.
- Reason 2 (competitive dynamics): Larger banks, fintech platforms or AI-native competitors could challenge the platform once the concept is proven.
- Reason 3 (financial logic): Customer acquisition, liquidity creation and trust-building would be expensive, which makes payback slower and launch risk higher.

---

## Section 7. First move

What is the one specific action the client must take in the next 6 months?

- Action: Start with a narrow controlled pilot focused on one or two simple local event markets while securing early payment partnerships and testing user trust.
- Owner: Founder or CEO with Product Lead, Legal Counsel and Payments Lead
- Success metric: The pilot proves that users understand the product, payment partners are willing to support it and early markets can stay liquid and transparent.
- Investment required (order of magnitude): Medium to high investment for product build, legal support, payment partnerships, liquidity seeding, brand development and customer acquisition.

# Overall Recommendation

The recommended strategic choice is Option 1: TBC-Backed Regulatory Sandbox Pathway.

This is the strongest route because the platform’s main challenge is not only technology. The real challenge is trust, regulation, payment access, compliance and mainstream Georgian adoption. Option 1 solves these problems better than the other two options because it combines NBG approval, GEL settlement, TBC-backed credibility, payment rails and customer access.

Option 2 should remain the fallback if the GEL-settled sandbox model is blocked.

Option 3 should not be the starting strategy because it creates the highest trust-building burden, highest launch risk and most expensive customer acquisition path.
