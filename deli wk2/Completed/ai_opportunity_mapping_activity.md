# AI Opportunity Map v1

## Activity: AI Opportunity Mapping

## Purpose

This activity maps specific AI use cases to specific value chain activities and evaluates them on two dimensions:

1. Business value potential
2. Implementation readiness

The goal is to identify which AI opportunities can be deployed soon, which ones require investment, which ones are quick operational improvements, and which ones should remain future options.

For this project, AI should not first be used to predict market outcomes. The stronger strategic role of AI is to make the future event-contract platform safer, clearer, more transparent, and more credible for users, regulators, and banking partners.

---

# Source and Strategic Fit

This opportunity map is linked to the Three Horizons Framework and the corrected strategic direction of the project.

Useful source: [McKinsey - Three Horizons of Growth](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/enduring-ideas-the-three-horizons-of-growth)

The project’s preferred direction is a regulated, bank-backed, GEL-settled event-contract platform. Therefore, the AI opportunity map should support:

- Regulatory trust
- Clear event-resolution rules
- Responsible-use controls
- Market monitoring
- User education
- Data quality
- Payment and KYC readiness
- Partner and regulator confidence

The platform should not be positioned as gambling or as a crypto-first product. It should be positioned as a supervised financial marketplace that uses simple event contracts and trusted official data sources.

# Scoring Definitions

| Dimension | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business value potential | Strong impact on trust, risk control, customer adoption, compliance, or platform viability | Useful but not central to platform success | Limited impact or mostly supportive |
| Implementation readiness | Can be deployed with current or near-current infrastructure | Requires moderate investment, data integration, or partner coordination | Requires major regulatory, technical, or market infrastructure work |

Common mistake: scoring every opportunity as Medium. The purpose of this map is to force clear priorities.

# Priority Use Cases

## Priority 1: AI-Assisted Market Explanations  
### Quadrant: Deploy Now

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Business value | High |
| Implementation readiness | High |
| Value chain activity | Customer engagement, education, market transparency |
| AI capabilities | Generation, synthesis |
| Evidence | The future platform will require simple user explanations because event contracts may be unfamiliar to mainstream Georgian users. Clear explanations reduce confusion and help separate the product from gambling-style speculation. |
| Barrier | The main barrier is not technical. The main challenge is making explanations legally careful, simple, and consistent with regulatory positioning. |

## Description

AI-assisted explanations would help users understand what each event contract means, what the risk is, how outcomes are resolved, and why the platform is different from betting or unregulated speculation.

For example, if the platform lists an event connected to GEL/USD exchange rates, the AI layer can explain the event, the official data source, the resolution rule, the risk, and the meaning of the market-implied probability in simple language.

## Two-Sentence Business Case

AI-assisted market explanations create value by reducing user confusion, support requests, and reputational risk while improving trust in the platform. The financial impact is indirect but important: a clearer user experience can improve activation, reduce support costs, and make the product more acceptable to regulators and banking partners.

---

## Priority 2: Market Monitoring and Integrity Prototype  
### Quadrant: Deploy Now

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Business value | High |
| Implementation readiness | High to Medium |
| Value chain activity | Risk control, market operations, compliance preparation |
| AI capabilities | Prediction, synthesis, anomaly detection |
| Evidence | A future event-contract platform must prove that markets are fair, outcomes are transparent, and suspicious behavior can be identified early. |
| Barrier | The main barrier is access to clean test data and clear definitions of suspicious or manipulative behavior. |

## Description

A market monitoring and integrity prototype would not require a full public launch. It could start as an internal tool or simulated-market dashboard that monitors test markets, flags unusual trading patterns, tracks liquidity, and checks whether outcome rules are understandable and reliable.

This is important because the platform should look more like a supervised financial marketplace than a normal fintech app. Market integrity must be designed before real-money trading is opened to users.

## Two-Sentence Business Case

Market monitoring creates value by helping the organisation detect unusual trading behavior, weak liquidity, unclear event rules, and potential manipulation risks before public launch. The financial impact comes from risk prevention: it reduces the probability of regulatory rejection, partner-bank hesitation, user disputes, and reputational damage.

# Invest to Enable Use Cases

## Use Case 3: Regulated Event-Contract Platform  
### Quadrant: Invest to Enable

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Business value | High |
| Implementation readiness | Medium |
| Value chain activity | Platform core, market operations, payments, compliance |
| AI capabilities | Prediction, generation, synthesis, agent workflow |
| Evidence | No clear regulated event-contract platform currently appears to operate in Georgia. The corrected strategic direction identifies white space for a GEL-settled, bank-backed product that combines trust, payment rails, responsible-use controls, and transparent event rules. |
| Barrier | Regulatory classification remains the main barrier. The platform must clarify whether the product is treated as a financial instrument, gambling-related activity, VASP-related activity, or a new regulated category. |

## Description

The regulated event-contract platform is the central long-term opportunity. It should allow users to participate in simple event contracts based on verifiable Georgian events, but only after legal classification, sandbox testing, payment-partner approval, and market-integrity controls are in place.

The recommended path is the TBC-backed regulatory sandbox pathway. This gives the platform a stronger chance of building trust, accessing GEL payment rails, and separating itself from gambling or crypto-first positioning.

Useful source: [NBG - Regulatory Sandbox Guide](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/page/regulatory-sandbox-guide)

## Use Case 4: Open-Banking-Powered Personalization  
### Quadrant: Invest to Enable

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Business value | High |
| Implementation readiness | Medium |
| Value chain activity | Customer data, payments, KYC, personalization |
| AI capabilities | Synthesis, prediction, generation, agent workflow |
| Evidence | Open banking can help financial institutions build more connected digital services by using consent-based financial data and payment initiation infrastructure. |
| Barrier | Implementation depends on API maturity, customer consent management, bank participation, and regulatory compliance. |

## Description

Open-banking-powered personalization can help the organisation understand user behavior, spending patterns, financial needs, and risk signals. For the future platform, this can support safer onboarding, responsible-use limits, payment flows, customer suitability checks, and personalized financial education.

This use case should be treated as an enabling capability rather than a standalone product. Without strong data infrastructure and customer-consent systems, the event-contract platform would be harder to operate safely.

Useful source: [NBG - Open Banking and Non-Banking Institutions](https://nbg.gov.ge/en/media/news/nbg-invites-non-banking-institutions-to-open-banking)

# Quick Wins

## Use Case 5: Data Cleaning and Fraud Detection  
### Quadrant: Quick Win

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Business value | Medium |
| Implementation readiness | High |
| Value chain activity | Data foundation, payment monitoring, risk control |
| AI capabilities | Prediction, synthesis, anomaly detection |
| Evidence | Georgia’s financial sector is increasingly digital, so payment security, data quality, and monitoring are important foundations for fintech development. |
| Barrier | The main barrier is internal data quality and access across systems. |

## Description

This use case improves operational reliability by identifying duplicate records, suspicious transactions, inconsistent data, and unusual user behavior. It is a practical first step because it strengthens the data and risk-control foundation needed for later platform development.

## Use Case 6: Customer Service Automation  
### Quadrant: Quick Win

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Business value | Medium |
| Implementation readiness | High |
| Value chain activity | Customer support, education, onboarding |
| AI capabilities | Generation, synthesis, agent workflow |
| Evidence | Digital financial products require fast, clear, and consistent support, especially when the product is new or unfamiliar to users. |
| Barrier | The main barrier is creating accurate Georgian-language responses and making sure sensitive cases are escalated to human agents. |

## Description

Customer service automation can support users with basic questions, product explanations, onboarding guidance, and support ticket routing. However, it should not fully replace human support, especially for complaints, financial disputes, settlement issues, or risk-related cases.

For the future event-contract platform, this use case helps prepare users for a product category that may be new in Georgia.

|---|---|
| Business value | Medium |

## Use Case 8: Regulatory Compliance Automation  
### Quadrant: Future Option

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Business value | Medium |
| Implementation readiness | Medium to Low |
| Value chain activity | Compliance, reporting, governance |
| AI capabilities | Synthesis, prediction, generation, agent workflow |
| Evidence | NBG’s fintech innovation direction and regulatory sandbox activity show that regulation is becoming more important for new financial products. |
| Barrier | Georgian reporting standards and product classifications may evolve, so automation should not be built too early without a clear regulatory framework. |

## Description

Regulatory compliance automation could help track regulatory updates, summarize requirements, prepare reporting drafts, monitor compliance deadlines, and support internal governance. This use case becomes more valuable after sandbox engagement and once the platform’s regulatory category is clearer.

# Summary of Priority Logic

The strongest AI priorities are not the most futuristic ones. The strongest priorities are the ones that make the platform more credible.

## Deploy Now priorities

1. AI-assisted market explanations
2. Market monitoring and integrity prototype

These can be developed early because they improve trust, transparency, and readiness without requiring a full public launch.

## Invest to Enable priorities

1. Regulated event-contract platform
2. Open-banking-powered personalization

These are strategically important but require regulatory, technical, and partner readiness before they can scale.

## Quick Wins

1. Data cleaning and fraud detection
2. Customer service automation

These improve current operations and prepare the organisation for more advanced AI use later.

## Future Options

1. AI-powered SME credit scoring
2. Regulatory compliance automation

These may become useful later, but they are not the immediate core of the event-contract platform strategy.

---

# Final Interpretation

The AI Opportunity Map shows that the project should use AI in a careful and staged way. The first priority is not to use AI for aggressive prediction or speculation. The first priority is to use AI to build trust, improve data quality, explain markets clearly, monitor risk, and support regulatory credibility.

The best near-term opportunities are AI-assisted market explanations and a market monitoring prototype. These support the corrected strategic direction because they make the platform easier to understand and safer to test.

The most important longer-term opportunities are the regulated event-contract platform and open-banking-powered personalization. These require more investment because they depend on regulatory comfort, payment rails, trusted data, customer consent, and partner readiness.

Overall, AI should be treated as the infrastructure that makes the platform credible, not just as a feature that predicts outcomes.
