# Activity: Strategic Options Analysis

## Purpose

Evaluate three structurally distinct strategic options using a consistent criteria matrix and produce a defended recommendation.

## Output

Strategic Options Analysis v1

## Time

40 minutes

---

## Option Descriptions

### TBC-Backed Regulatory Sandbox Pathway

This option positions the platform as a serious financial event-contract tool launched with TBC’s trust, payment rails, compliance capacity and customer access. The platform would seek NBG sandbox approval before public launch, settle contracts in GEL and focus on measurable macroeconomic and civic outcomes such as exchange-rate movements or local election outcomes. This is the most mainstream route because it combines regulatory credibility, bank-backed distribution and a clear trust layer for Georgian users.

### VASP-Registered Crypto-Native Platform

This option launches the platform as a VASP-registered crypto-native business using USDC or stablecoin settlement. Event contracts would be positioned as prediction tokens or convertible virtual assets, with users mainly coming from crypto-native communities. This route may allow faster launch and more flexible settlement, but the audience would be narrower and direct GEL integration would be more difficult.

### Standalone Fintech Launch

This option launches the platform as an independent Georgian fintech brand without direct bank backing at the beginning. The company would build its own customer base, payment partnerships, liquidity strategy, compliance process and regulatory relationship from zero. This gives the platform more independence and product control, but it also creates higher launch risk, slower trust-building and more expensive customer acquisition.

---

## Criteria Matrix

| Criteria | TBC-Backed Regulatory Sandbox Pathway | Crypto-Native VASP | Standalone Fintech Launch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic fit | High: This option directly solves the trust and regulation problem by using TBC’s credibility and NBG sandbox approval. | Medium: It solves the speed-to-market problem, but does not fully solve mainstream trust and local adoption. | Medium: It supports independence, but does not strongly solve the initial trust and regulatory credibility problem. |
| Financial return | High: TBC’s existing customer base, payment rails and distribution can reduce acquisition cost and improve scale potential. | Medium: Launch may be faster and cheaper, but the addressable audience is smaller because it mainly targets crypto-native users. | Low: The platform would need to spend more on customer acquisition, payment partnerships, liquidity and trust-building. |
| Implementation feasibility | Medium: TBC already has useful capabilities, but NBG approval and compliance structuring still make implementation complex. | High: A crypto-native model may be easier to launch technically because it can use stablecoin settlement and VASP partners. | Low: Building the product, liquidity, customer base, partnerships and regulatory relationship from zero is operationally difficult. |
| Competitive defensibility | High: A TBC-backed structure creates strong trust, distribution, compliance and local market advantages against new entrants. | Medium: Crypto-native competitors could copy the model and differentiation would depend mostly on speed, liquidity and user experience. | Low: Without a strong trust layer or large distribution partner, the platform would be easier for larger fintech or AI-native competitors to challenge. |

## Financial Logic

### TBC-Backed Regulatory Sandbox Pathway

The value creation logic comes from combining a new event-contract marketplace with TBC’s existing trust, payment infrastructure, compliance strength and customer access. Revenue can come from transaction fees, spreads or platform participation fees as users trade simple GEL-settled event contracts. The key financial assumption is that bank-backed credibility will significantly lower customer acquisition cost and increase adoption among mainstream Georgian users.

### VASP-Registered Crypto-Native Platform

The value creation logic comes from launching faster through stablecoin settlement and targeting users who already understand crypto-based products. Revenue can come from trading fees, tokenized event-contract activity and liquidity-provider participation. The key financial assumption is that the crypto-native user base will be large and active enough to create sustainable liquidity, even without strong direct access to mainstream GEL users.

### Standalone Fintech Launch

The value creation logic comes from building an independent fintech platform with full control over product design, branding, pricing and growth strategy. Revenue can come from trading fees and future partnerships if the platform successfully builds user trust and market liquidity. The key financial assumption is that the company can acquire users, build liquidity and gain regulatory confidence without spending too much time or capital before reaching scale.

---

## Recommendation

The recommended option is the TBC-Backed Regulatory Sandbox Pathway. This is the strongest strategic choice because the platform’s main challenge is not only technology, but trust, regulation, distribution and credibility. First, TBC’s trust gives the product bank-level legitimacy in a market where users may be cautious about event-contract platforms. Second, TBC’s payment rails, compliance capacity and customer access make mainstream Georgian adoption more realistic than a crypto-native or standalone model. Third, GEL settlement and NBG sandbox approval create a safer and more locally relevant structure than stablecoin-only or independent launch options. The recommendation would change only if NBG clearly refuses to approve the sandbox structure or classifies the product as gambling. In that case, the VASP-registered crypto-native route would become the fallback option.

---

## Final Recommendation

Choose the TBC-Backed Regulatory Sandbox Pathway as the core path.

The VASP-Registered Crypto-Native Platform should remain a fallback route if regulation blocks the GEL-settled sandbox model.

The Standalone Fintech Launch should not be the starting strategy, but it can become useful later if the platform proves demand and wants more independence.
