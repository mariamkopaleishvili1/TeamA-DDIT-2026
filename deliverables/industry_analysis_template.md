## Part 1. Industry definition

- Industry name: Broad Fintech, Specializing in Prediction Markets
- Geographic scope: Georgia 
- Main product or service categories: Decentralized, crypto-based prediction market platform
- Time horizon under consideration: 1 Year

### Boundary note
What is included in the industry analysis, and what is excluded?

This analysis includes Georgian retail fintech apps, VASP-registered crypto exchanges, and digital banking payment rails. It excludes traditional corporate lending, physical real estate, and unregulated offshore gambling sites. Global perspective: Similar to Kalshi in the US, success requires strict boundary separation from traditional sports betting to appease local financial regulators.

## Part 2. Market structure

- Approximate market size: The Georgian digital payments market reached approximately $2.31 billion in 2024 [BTUAI](https://btuai.ge/en/fintech-market-perspectives-in-georgia-a-new-era-of-digital-finance/). The broader fintech sector is projected to exceed $2 billion by 2027, with digital commerce and mPOS payments as primary growth drivers. Currently, approximately 50 fintech companies operate in Georgia, with the Georgian Fintech Association uniting 21 member companies as of 2022 [BTU](https://btu.edu.ge/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Fintech-market-in-Georgia-structure-and-main-trends.pdf).
- Growth rate or trajectory: The digital payments segment is experiencing robust growth with a projected CAGR of approximately 10.91% through 2028 [Yucatan Magazine](https://yucatanmagazine.com/georgias-e-payment-market-to-rise-by-10-91-till-2028/). Online financial transactions grew 25.6% year-over-year in 2022, reaching 8.16 billion GEL in transaction volume [BTU](https://btu.edu.ge/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Fintech-market-in-Georgia-structure-and-main-trends.pdf). The National Bank of Georgia has positioned fintech development as a strategic priority for establishing Georgia as a regional fintech hub along the Middle Corridor [GBC](https://www.gbc.ge/en/news/finanse/nbg-presents-fintech-strategy-and-regulatory-vision-to-fintech-association-member-companies).
- Major segments:
   - Digital Payments & Mobile Wallets: Cards (Visa/Mastercard) dominate with 89% of e-purchases; mobile wallets represent emerging growth [Yucatan Magazine](https://yucatanmagazine.com/georgias-e-payment-market-to-rise-by-10-91-till-2028/)
   - Alternative Lending: Data-driven and AI-powered credit solutions for SMEs and individuals [BTUAI](https://btuai.ge/en/fintech-market-perspectives-in-georgia-a-new-era-of-digital-finance/)
   - Investment Platforms: Early-stage development with TBC Capital and Galt & Taggart providing access to 36-60 global exchanges 
   - Cryptocurrency & VASP Services: Regulated sector with mandatory NBG registration since July 2023 
   - Open Banking: Phase 1 launched in 2021, with non-bank inclusion regulations issued in 2023 [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2023/10/02/digital-payments-in-georgia-bolstering-the-regulatory-environment-and-developing-infrastructure-for-an-inclusive-future)
- Important structural differences across segments:
   - Regulatory maturity varies significantly: Payments and VASPs have clear regulatory frameworks; prediction markets remain unregulated and undefined
   - Bank vs. non-bank competition: Traditional banks (TBC, Bank of Georgia) operate fintech subsidiaries with inherent trust advantages
   - International vs. local players: International fintechs (Paysera, United Payment) entering market alongside local innovators
   - Urban-rural digital divide: Tbilisi and major cities show high digital adoption; rural areas remain cash-dependent [PayAtlas](https://payatlas.com/countries/georgia-ge)
