# Disruption Diagnostic Worksheet

## Purpose

This worksheet helps teams evaluate whether a market shift fits Christensen's disruption logic or should be classified differently.

Do not force every case into "disruption." Precision matters.

---

## Part 1. Define the incumbent market

- Industry: Fintech
- Main incumbent firms: TBC Capital, Bank of Georgia Brokerage, Galt & Taggart, crypto exchanges used informally (e.g., Binance, Coinbase), and local betting companies (partial overlap).
- Traditional business model: Brokerages rely on trading commissions, custody fees, FX spread, and larger minimum trade sizes. Banks rely on deposit gathering, wealth products, portfolio management fees, and conservative financial instruments.
Retail speculation requires significant starting capital, financial literacy, and complex onboarding
- Primary basis of competition before the challenger appeared:
  Trust in banking brands
  Access to listed equities/bonds
  Research, analysis, and advisor‑driven investing
  Trading tools & “serious investor” positioning
  Barriers: literacy, minimum capital, account requirements

---

## Part 2. Define the challenger or technology shift

- Challenger firm or technology: A Polymarket/Kalshi‑style prediction‑market platform offering event‑based, $1 outcome contracts.
- Initial offering:
  Micro‑contracts (as low as $1)
  Zero trading fees (revenue via bid–ask spreads)
  Liquid event markets (elections, inflation prints, sports, crypto events)
  Fast onboarding + intuitive yes/no structure
- Initial target users:
  Young (18–35), tech‑savvy Georgians
  Non‑consumers of traditional brokerage (those priced out or intimidated by stocks)
  Sports bettors, crypto users, and news‑driven speculators
  Low‑capital individuals seeking simple speculation instruments
- Why it seemed interesting or threatening:
  Wide range of trading topics
  Removes complexity of stock investing
  Reduces cost by 70–90% vs. traditional brokerage
  Opens trading access to non‑consumers
  Challenges the incumbents’ future retail growth engine

### Question 3
Did incumbents have a rational reason to ignore the entrant at first?

|              |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Answer**   | **Yes**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Evidence** | Incumbents have three rational reasons to ignore prediction markets initially: (1) Regulatory uncertainty: NBG has authority to define virtual asset services but has not classified event contracts, creating compliance risk that banks avoid. (2) Brand positioning: TBC Capital and Bank of Georgia position as "serious" wealth management brands; event contracts resemble gambling, conflicting with trust-based brand equity. (3) Economic disincentive: Incumbents earn from custody fees, portfolio management, and deposit gathering. Prediction markets generate trading velocity but not AUM, threatening the core business model rather than enhancing it. Galt & Taggart's \$1,000 minimum and TBC Capital's recent \$1 fractional launch show incumbents focus on equity ownership, not event speculation. |

## Part 4. Performance trajectory

| Question                                           | Answer                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Did the entrant improve over time?**             | Too early to assess in Georgia (market does not exist as of March 2026). Global precedent: Polymarket improved from \$1M to \$500M+ monthly volume (2024-2025) by adding CFTC-regulated Kalshi acquisition, oracle reliability, and political event liquidity.                                                  |
| **Did it move toward the mainstream market?**      | Globally, yes: Polymarket/Kalshi moved from crypto-native users to mainstream retail during 2024 US election cycle. In Georgia, mainstream would mean capturing banked customers from TBC Capital's \$1 fractional investing, requiring product evolution beyond price.                                         |
| **What changed in cost, convenience, or quality?** | Cost: Zero commission became standard (TBC Capital matched). Convenience: Sub-2-minute onboarding vs. days for brokerage. Quality: Oracle resolution transparency vs. advisor-driven research. Global evolution: CFTC regulation (Kalshi) improved trust quality; settlement speed improved from days to hours. |

---
Evidence:
TBC Capital's October 2023 launch of zero-commission, $1 fractional investing shows incumbents can match cost quickly. The sustainable trajectory must shift to convenience (onboarding speed) and quality (local event relevance, short-term alignment). Global prediction markets improved through regulatory legitimacy (CFTC approval) and oracle reliability, not just price cuts.

## Part 5. Classification

Choose the strongest classification:
[ ] Low-end disruption
[x] New-market disruption
[ ] Sustaining innovation
[ ] Architectural or platform shift that does not fit Christensen cleanly
[ ] Too early to classify
Justification
This is new-market disruption, not low-end disruption. The critical distinction lies in who gets served and why incumbents rationally ignore the entrant. Georgian brokerage incumbents (TBC Capital, Galt & Taggart) did not overserve low-end customers on price; TBC Capital already offers zero-commission, $1 fractional investing. Instead, they failed to serve non-consumers at all: the 92% of Georgians not participating in retail brokerage despite 54% financial literacy capability. The barrier was never price alone but product-behavior mismatch. Incumbent brokerage products are designed for long-term wealth accumulation (equities, bonds), while 66% of Georgians prefer short-term outcomes. Prediction markets create a new market by redefining the job-to-be-done from "invest in companies" to "speculate on events," matching behavioral preferences that incumbents cannot address without compromising their trust-based, "serious investor" brand positioning. Incumbents rationally ignore this because (1) regulatory classification is uncertain, (2) the business model threatens rather than complements their AUM-driven revenue, and (3) event contracts superficially resemble gambling, conflicting with bank brand equity. This mirrors Christensen's new-market disruption pattern: creating access for non-consumers by competing against non-consumption itself, not against incumbent offerings. The prediction market does not win by being cheaper than TBC Capital (it is not, at the margin); it wins by being more intuitive for the 66% short-term preference population that TBC Capital's 6,000-stock platform cannot reach.
